# The 6-Block Convention: Santa-Only or Broader?

**Question:** The six Santa quipu group pendants in same-color blocks of 6
(mean 85.9% of pendants in 6-blocks). Is this a Santa-only scribal signature,
or does it appear elsewhere?

**Method:** Full-population check. For all 611 quipu with level-1 pendant color
data in OKR, calculated the fraction of pendants falling in same-color runs of
exactly 6, in CORD_ORDINAL order. Grouped by provenience.

**Corpus baseline:** Mean 5.4%, median 0%. 6-blocking is rare overall.

## Answer: Not Santa-only, but Santa is unique in consistency

### Santa stands alone as a site

All six Santa quipu are 67–100% 6-blocked (mean 85.9%). No other site with
n≥2 comes close. The next-highest multi-quipu sites are Ullujaya/Ocucaje/Ica
(34%, n=2) and Costa Sur (20%, n=2) — both driven by single outliers.

### But 7 other large quipu exceed 50%

Among quipu with ≥50 pendants (n=221), 13 exceed 50% 6-blocking. Six are
the Santa set. The other seven:

| Quipu | Site | 6-block % | Pendants | Form | Pattern |
|-------|------|-----------|----------|------|---------|
| 1000121 | Ica | 70.9% | 110 | E | Perfect alternating YB/B 6-blocks |
| 1000563 | Ullujaya, Ocucaje, Ica | 68.4% | 79 | C | Opens 6-blocked, then breaks down |
| 1000315 | Pachacamac | 63.6% | 66 | E | Mixed 6-blocks and other sizes |
| 1000293 | Paracas | 62.1% | 145 | F | 6-blocks interspersed |
| 1000230 | (none) | 59.0% | 61 | E | 6-blocks among many singletons |
| 1000345 | (none) | 55.2% | 87 | F | Mixed |
| 1000544 | unknown | 55.1% | 98 | B | Weak — mostly 1s and 2s |

### The Ica case is the strongest non-Santa match

1000121 (Ica, Museum für Völkerkunde Berlin) shows perfect alternating
YB/B blocks of 6 — the same clean layout as Santa. It is Form E (like the
Santa six), has 2 structural levels (110 pendants + 13 subsidiaries), and
similar knot-type profile. This is not coincidence; it is the same
convention applied at a different office.

### Geographic pattern: scattered, not clustered

The non-Santa 6-blocked quipu come from:
- Ica region (south coast): 1000121, 1000563
- Pachacamac (central coast): 1000315
- Paracas (south coast): 1000293
- Unknown provenience: 1000230, 1000345, 1000544

Santa is on the north-central coast (Ancash). The other cases are NOT
geographically adjacent — they span the south coast, central coast, and
unknown origins. There is no regional cluster around Santa.

They are also in different museums (Berlin, Munich, Goteborg, Lima) with
independent collection histories — not a single looted lot.

### Small-quipu caveat

Several small quipu (≤10 pendants) hit 100% 6-blocking trivially — a quipu
with exactly 6 same-color pendants is 100% by definition. The meaningful
signal is in large quipu with sustained 6-blocking across many blocks,
which is what Santa shows and what 1000121 (Ica) replicates.

## Interpretation

The 6-block is **not Santa-exclusive**, but Santa is the only site where it
is the **consistent office standard** rather than an isolated occurrence.

Two models fit the data:

1. **Shared scribal repertoire.** The 6-block was a known layout option in
   the broader Inca bureaucratic toolkit. Most offices used it rarely or
   never; the Santa office adopted it as standard; the Ica office used it
   for at least one document. The scattered outliers are independent
   adoptions.

2. **Functional trigger.** Something about the *content* (not the site)
   calls for 6-blocking — e.g., recording in groups of 6 (tribute units,
   work teams, decimal-adjacent grouping). Offices that handled such
   content used 6-blocks; Santa handled it routinely.

The Ica parallel (Form E, 2 levels, clean alternating 6-blocks) favors
model 1 — same convention, different office. But the functional question
remains open: *why* 6?

## Bottom line for Charles

The Santa 6-block is real and distinctive as a **site-level standard** —
no other office used it this consistently. But it is not a Santa invention;
it appears as isolated cases from Ica to Pachacamac to Paracas. It belongs
to the shared Inca scribal repertoire, not to one office's private code.
The stronger claim ("Santa signature") holds for *consistency of use*,
not for *exclusive knowledge* of the convention.
