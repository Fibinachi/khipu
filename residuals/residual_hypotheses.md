# Residual Hypotheses: What Are the Unclassified?

## H1: Regional paperwork dialects (STRONGEST)

Rare knot types map to regions, not random noise:

| Knot | Region | Khipu |
|---|---|---|
| SP (spiral?) | Incahuasi (4) + South Coast Ica/Pisco/Ocucaje (3) | 1000504, 1000514, 1000526, 1000528, 1000533, 1000554, 1000598, 1000605, 1000608, 1000613 |
| LL | Lluta Valley | 1000096 (+3 others) |
| TF (tufted) | Ica/Pisco | 1000522 (unary tally system) |
| S-twist dominance | Costa Sur, Paracas, Nazca, Ica, Mollepampa | 38 khipu |

These are not "failed normal khipu." They are non-standard regional paperwork.
The Cuzco-centric classification was trained on central-coast norms; south-coast
and valley-specific offices used their own knot/direction conventions.

**Testable prediction:** excavated khipu from Incahuasi should show SP knots;
from Lluta should show LL; from south coast should show S-twist dominance.

## H2: Dense-value document type (the 9-group)

1000265, 1000266, 1000267, 1000268, 1000269, 1000344, 1000443, 1000446, 1000608.

- Only 9% zero-value cords vs 61% residual median (91% nonzero!)
- 4 colors, 72 cords, S-twist heavy, sequential IDs (same collection)
- 5 have no provenance; S-twist predicts south-coast origin

A khipu where nearly every cord carries a value has minimal structural overhead.
Either a distinct document type (dense ledger?) or a regional style that does not
use zero-padding the way central-coast khipu do.

**Not damage:** decode rate is 100%. These are complete, deliberate, information-dense.

## H3: Stubs and fragments (23-group)

32 median cords, 4 colors, 95% zeros. Tiny khipu that are almost entirely empty.
Probably incomplete records, practice pieces, or minimal single-entry documents.
Not a document type; a preservation/completeness category.

## H4: Funerary contamination (5 khipu)

1000004, 1000005, 1000008, 1000009, 1000018 (Leymebamba). These entered via the
anomalous-transcription set, which deliberately mixed funerary and non-funerary.
They should be EXCLUDED from administrative residual analysis, not treated as
unclassified admin documents. Their "weirdness" is genre, not anomaly.

## H5: Twist-anomalous cluster (44-group)

U-dominant and S-dominant khipu including Nazca, unknown-provenance, and the
all-U office pieces. Twist proportions in the residual set (S 26%, U 20%) are
IDENTICAL to the full corpus (S 24%, U 21%). Twist dominance is not what makes
these unclassified; it is a corpus-wide phenomenon. This group needs the
moiety-vs-register-vs-office disambiguation, not residual analysis.

## H6: Classified-but-weird (18 khipu)

13 census-like + 4 warehouse + 1 warehouse-like with rare/anomalous features.
The supervised classifier assigned them, but their rare knots or twist patterns
suggest regional manufacture. Classification by structure, anomaly by execution:
a census-form khipu tied by a south-coast office using SP knots.

## H7: True residuals

After removing H1-H6, approximately 40-50 khipu remain genuinely unclassified:
no rare knots, normal twist, no clear sub-cluster, supervised classifier abstained.
These may be:
- Damaged normal khipu (missing cords/colors distort features)
- Rare document types with n too small to cluster
- Individual scribal variation within known types
- Palimpsests or reused khipu

## Verdict

The "unclassified" are not a population. They are at least five different things:
regional dialects (H1), a possible dense type (H2), stubs (H3), funerary (H4),
and true unknowns (H7). The rare features are the most systematic signal in the
entire residual set: they are regional, not random. "Weird symbols" = local office
conventions. The classification did not fail on these khipu; it was trained on
the wrong geography.
