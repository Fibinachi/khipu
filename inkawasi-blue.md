# Inkawasi Blue Cords: Which Quipu, What Context

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Question:** Which Inkawasi quipu have blue cords, and do they overlap with the commodity-mapped quipu?

## Summary

**9 of 52** Inkawasi quipu have blue-family cords. **Zero overlap** with the ten commodity-mapped quipu (beans/chili/peanuts). The blue quipu are a completely separate set.

## Archaeological pattern

The blue quipu cluster in **two groups**:

1. **Early excavations** (UR262, UR264): Less precise provenience. UR264/UR265 were found tied together — two quipu linked by a single cord, suggesting they function as a pair.

2. **Sector B, 2016 excavations** (JC004 through JC023): Precise provenience. Most are from **UA 07** (Unidad Arquitectónica 7). Several are physically attached to or bundled with other quipu (JC004↔JC005, JC011 bundle with JC012/JC013).

The commodity-mapped quipu (beans/chili/peanuts) were found in the **SW corner, kallanka U.A. 4** — a different architectural unit from the blue quipu.

## Interpretation

Blue at Inkawasi does **not** mark the known commodities. The blue quipu are:
- From different rooms/areas than the commodity quipu
- Often physically linked to other quipu (pairs, bundles)
- Not associated with any excavated product

This is consistent with the "state context" hypothesis: blue marks imperial administrative records (paired/bundled quipu for cross-checking?) rather than specific goods. The commodity quipu (beans/chili/peanuts) use the coastal palette without blue because they're recording local products, not state-level administration.

Alternatively, blue may mark highland-origin goods that arrived without their original commodity context preserved — quipu that traveled with products but were stored separately from them.

## What's needed

The UR262/UR264 (1000603/1000605) excavation notes may contain commodity associations not captured in the OKR. These are from the earliest excavations and have the most blue cords. Checking the original field records for these two would be the highest-value follow-up.
