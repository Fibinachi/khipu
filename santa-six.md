# The Six Santa Quipu: What They Share Beyond Form Type

All six Santa quipu (OKR 1000270–1000275, Museo Temple Radicati, museum nos. 1–6)
are Form E (hierarchical ledger). Analysis of what else they share, and whether
their differences look systematic.

## Shared traits (beyond Form E)

1. **Exactly two structural levels.** All have pendants (level 1) plus
   subsidiaries (level 2), never level 3. Form E elsewhere includes deeper
   hierarchies; the Santa six are uniformly shallow.

2. **Same value scale.** Max per-cord value (simplified knot proxy) is 9–15 in
   all six. No quipu records large numbers. They all count small quantities.

3. **The 6-block convention — a Santa signature.** Pendants are grouped in
   color blocks of 6: 1000271 and 1000272 are perfectly 6-blocked (15 and 10
   blocks); the others are 24–68% 6-blocked. In a sample of 15 other Form E
   quipu, the mean 6-block fraction is **1.4%** (max 10%). The 6-block is not
   a Form E feature — it is a local scribal convention, distinctive to this
   office or region.

4. **Pendant counts are multiples of 6** in five of six (54, 90, 60, 354, 120).
   The documents are laid out on a 6-grid. Only 1000275 (206) breaks it.

5. **Zero E-E-E markers** on all six.

6. **Subsidiaries do not sum to parents** (0–19% match). These are not
   subtotal hierarchies in the simple sense; the level-2 cords are detail
   entries, not arithmetic breakdowns.

7. **Plain primary cords** (struct=P) in all six; consecutive museum numbers
   1–6 — one acquisition lot, same collection context. No excavation data
   (discoverer/date blank).

## Do the differences encode a series? Answer: no clean code found.

- No monotonic progression in totals, block counts, palette size, or cord
  counts vs. catalog order (9/15/10/59/20 blocks; 475/359/176/619/655/967).
- Five of six have a color unique within the set (CB, VG, BY+LA, B, BL+GL),
  but 1000272 has no unique color — it is distinguished only by small size
  and the shortest primary cord (38.5 vs 123–162.5).
- Opening-block colors are 5 distinct of 6 (270 and 272 both open LG).
- Two are recto-heavy, three verso-heavy, one mixed — but the recto pair
  (272/273) are wildly different sizes (60 vs 354 pendants), unlike the
  Inkawasi JC004/JC005 mirror pair.

The differences look like **different-sized ledgers from one office using one
set of conventions** — different accounts, periods, or batches — not a
recoverable sequential code. The shared conventions (6-grid, 2 levels, small
values, no E-E-E) say "same bureaucratic office"; the variation (size,
palette accent, attachment) distinguishes the documents without encoding an
obvious series number.

## Distinctive within Form E?

Yes. The Santa six are the most 6-blocked quipu in the corpus. Whatever Form E
means functionally, the Santa office filled it out with its own formatting
grammar. This is the strongest site-level scribal signature found so far —
stronger than any single color, because it is a *layout* convention, not a
palette choice.

## Limitations

- No excavation context (all museum data blank except numbers).
- Values use simplified knot decoding (no positional multipliers); exact
  decimal values not recovered.
- 6-block comparison sampled 15 of 104 other Form E quipu; full-population
  check would firm up the "Santa signature" claim.
