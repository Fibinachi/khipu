# Quechua Reference Grammar for Khipu Decipherment

**Purpose:** Published grammatical descriptions of Quechua, organized for use as the reference grammar in mapping khipu structures to language. Priority: Cuzco–Collao (Southern Quechua, Quechua IIC) — the variety closest to the Inca administrative language.

**Principle (researcher's):** Words change; grammar is stable. These are published descriptions, not reconstructions.

**Key typological facts:** Agglutinative, SOV (flexible), nominative-accusative, pro-drop, no gender, no articles. Morphological template: `ROOT-derivational-inflectional=enclitics` (Faller, SALT 2002).

---

## 1. Evidentiality

The most-studied Quechua grammatical system. Evidentiality is marked by **enclitics** (not suffixes) that attach at the clause edge. They are mutually exclusive within a clause and cannot occur under negation or in non-illocutionary environments.

| Enclitic | Label | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| =mi (allomorph -n) | Direct / Best Possible Grounds | Speaker has the best available evidence | *Parashan=mi* — "It is raining" (I see it) |
| =si (allomorph -s) | Reportative | Acquired via report from others | *Parashan=si* — "It is raining" (I was told) |
| =chá | Conjectural | Inference from reasoning or speculation | *Parashan=chá* — "It is raining" (I infer) |
| =chusina / =(chu)-sina | Perceived Evidence Inferential | Inference from observable results | Used when inferring from visible traces |

**Key details (Faller 2002, UMass dissertation; Faller, SALT 2002):**
- =mi is NOT limited to direct perception. It marks "best possible grounds" — the best source a normal person would have for that kind of information. This includes trusted reports integrated into the speaker's knowledge, encyclopedic facts, and even emotional states of others when reported by the person themselves.
- =si is purely evidential (marks hearsay); it does not encode epistemic judgment. It also has social uses: distancing, framing gossip, casting doubt (Babel 2009).
- =chá encodes BOTH evidential (inference) and epistemic (possibility) meaning.
- The evidentials are a subset of **focus enclitics**: =mi also marks focus (the answer to a wh-question). Evidentiality and information structure are intertwined.
- CQ has a **second evidential system in the past tense**: -rqa (witnessed past) vs -sqa (narrative/unwitnessed past). See §6.
- Acquisition order (Courtney 2015): -mi and -chá emerge age 2–3; -si not until age 4+. In Chuquisaca Quechua, -si has attrited before -mi (reverse of acquisition).

**Dialect note:** The tripartite =mi/=si/=chá system is most robustly described for Cuzco. Other varieties may have reduced systems (Weber 1989 on Huallaga; Adelaar 1977).

**Khipu mapping (hypothesized):** Color-marking intensity tracks evidential distance. Tested: marking rises monotonically with cord depth L1→L4 (p=1.5e-213). Confidence: **strong** (structural).

## 2. Switch-Reference

Dependent clauses mark whether their subject is the **same** (SS) or **different** (DS) from the main clause subject. This is how Quechua chains clauses in narrative.

| Variety | SS marker | DS marker | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cuzco–Collao | -spa | -qti / -pti | Cusihuamán 1976/2001 |
| Ancash (QI) | -r / -shpa | -pti | Cole 1983; Georgi (Leipzig) |
| Imbabura (Ecuador) | -shpa | -jpi | Cole 1983 |

**Key details:**
- SS-marked verbs do **not** show subject agreement (the T head is "defective"); DS-marked verbs **do** cross-reference the subject person.
- SS clauses are structurally smaller (more infinitive-like); DS clauses are fuller.
- In Cuzco Quechua, the SS nominalizer -spa also appears in reciprocal constructions (Faller, on reciprocity).
- Example (Imbabura, Cole 1983): *Utavaluman chayashpa, ñuka mamata rikurkani* — "When I arrived-SS in Otavalo, I saw my mother." vs *Juzi Utavaluman chayajpi, paypaj wasiman rirkani* — "When José arrived-DS in Otavalo, I went to his house."

**Khipu mapping (hypothesized):** Pendant-to-pendant binding (e.g., 1000344) may mark same/different source across linked records. Testable via color-sharing of linked pairs. Confidence: **suggestive**.

## 3. Nominalization

Quechua builds relative clauses and complement clauses through **nominalizers** — suffixes that turn verbs into nouns. There are no relative pronouns.

| Nominalizer | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -na | Obligative / concretive; complement of control verbs ("want") | *Rinayta munani* — "I want to go" (lit. "I want my going"); *kuchana* ("knife") from *kuchuy* ("to cut") |
| -sqa | Perfective participle; complement of non-control verbs; relative clauses | *Yachasqaykita ama qunqankichu* — "Don't forget what you learned" |
| -y | Infinitive | *Rimay* — "to speak" |
| -q | Agentive | *Pukllaq* — "player" (one who plays); also habitual past auxiliary |

**Key details:**
- Control verbs (want, try) take -na complements; other verbs take -sqa (Ríos, HyTra 2013).
- Relative clauses are **rare** in spontaneous speech (Courtney 2006: 4 in 20 hours). Three types: headless, externally-headed (most common), internally-headed.
- -sqa doubles as the narrative past tense marker (see §6).

**Khipu mapping (hypothesized):** Recursive pendant-through-subsidiary-knot (1000178) may parallel nominalized embedding. Confidence: **suggestive**.

## 4. Negation

Negation is a **circumfix**: the independent particle *mana* ("not") at the left edge + the suffix *-chu* on the negated verb.

- *Mana rinichu* — "I do not go" (not go-1-NEG)
- *Mana taytaytaqa rimarichisqakupaschu* — "They didn't let my father talk either" (Faller, SALT 2002, ex. 4b)

**Key details:**
- *mana* is a particle (indeclinable) but can take some suffixes: *manan/manam* (with -n/-m), *manaraq* ("not yet"), *manachu* ("or not?").
- *-chu* attaches to the verb (or the focused negated constituent).
- Evidential enclitics **cannot** occur under negation (Faller 2002) — negation blocks illocutionary force.

**Khipu mapping (hypothesized):** U-direction (reversed) knots may mark negation or correction. The mended upside-down pendant (1000201, all-U knots) supports a repair/negation reading. Confidence: **suggestive** (primary: repair marking).

## 5. Topic and Focus

Information structure is marked **morphologically**, not prosodically (O'Rourke 2005; Muntendam 2014).

| Marker | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -qa | Topic | *Mamayqa qheswata rin* — "My mother-TOP went to the valley" |
| -taq | Contrastive topic | *Taytaytaq chakrata* — "My father-CONTR to the field" |
| =mi / -n | Focus (= evidential, §1) | Marks the answer to a wh-question |
| =lla | Exclusive ("only") | *Domingolla* — "only on Sundays" |
| =pas | Additive ("too/even") | *Inkapas* — "the Incas too" |

**Key details:**
- -qa marks "discourse-accessible information of common concern" (Sánchez 2010, citing Pereltsvaig 2004).
- The evidential =mi doubles as a focus marker — the same morpheme does both jobs.
- Word order shifts with topic/focus: topicalized constituents move to clause margins.

**Khipu mapping (hypothesized):** Marker cords tied through knots (1000183) may function as focus markers — flagging target values. Signal threads (1000004/1000005) may parallel discourse enclitics. Confidence: **suggestive**.

## 6. Tense and Aspect

| Marker | Function |
|---|---|
| (unmarked) | Present |
| -rqa (~-ra) | Witnessed/experienced past |
| -sqa | Narrative/unwitnessed past (also perfective participle, §3) |
| -shaq / -nqa | Future (1st/2nd person -shaq; 3rd person -nqa) |
| -qka | Habitual past |
| -chka | Progressive |
| -na-...-ku | Reciprocal (not tense, but verbal) |

**Key details (Mannheim 1998, *American Anthropologist* 100(2)):**
- The **two past tenses** encode evidentiality: -rqa = speaker witnessed/participated; -sqa = speaker did not witness (narrative, "drunken past," hearsay past). Cusihuamán (1976) describes -sqa as marking "any action that has taken place either without the direct participation of the speaker, or while the speaker was not fully conscious."
- Tense marking is **obligatory** on finite verbs — every Quechua clause is tensed.
- Person agreement is **bipersonal**: verbs agree with both subject and object (*-wa-* = 1st person object, *-su-* = 2nd person object).

**Khipu mapping:** NO khipu correlate proposed. If khipu lack verbs (file-type-as-verb hypothesis), they may lack tense entirely — tenseless state descriptions. This is a **prediction**, not a finding.

## 7. Number System

Decimal, fully regular:

| Value | Quechua (Cuzco) |
|---|---|
| 1–9 | *huk, iskay, kimsa, tawa, pisqa, suqta, qanchis, pusaq, isqun* |
| 10 | *chunka* |
| 100 | *pachak* |
| 1,000 | *waranqa* |
| 1,000,000 | *hunu* |

**Key details:**
- The decimal structure is confirmed by khipu knot organization (units/tens/hundreds by position) — the number system and the khipu encoding are mutually confirming.
- No non-decimal remnants in the standard system.

**Khipu mapping:** Established. The Ascher decimal decoding is the confirmed numerical grammar.

## 8. Possession

Two-part system: **genitive case** on the possessor + **possessive suffix** on the possessed noun.

- Genitive: *-q* / *-pa* (e.g., *wasiq* — "of the house"; *Phuturiq wawan* — "Phuturi's child")
- Possessive suffixes: *-y* (my), *-yki* (your), *-n* (his/her/its), *-nchis* (our incl.), *-yku* (our excl.), *-ykichis* (your pl.), *-nku* (their)

Example: *wasiyki* = "your house"; *taytay* = "my father."

**Khipu mapping (hypothesized):** Subsidiary attachment may encode possession — "X's goods held by Y." The rider model (subsidiaries = others' goods on the house record) is structurally a possessive construction. Confidence: **suggestive**.

## 9. Word Order

- **Canonical: SOV.** *Allquqa kawalluta kanin* — "The dog-TOP the horse-ACC bites."
- **Actually flexible:** all six orders (SOV, SVO, OSV, OVS, VOS, VSO) are grammatical. Case marking (-ta accusative) preserves meaning across permutations. Word order signals **pragmatics** (topic/focus), not truth conditions (Cerrón-Palomino 1994).
- **Noun phrase order:** adjective–noun (*yana allqu* — "black dog"). Demonstrative–numeral–adjective–noun.
- **Postpositions**, not prepositions (consistent with SOV).
- **Pro-drop:** subjects and objects freely omitted when recoverable.

**Khipu mapping:** Cord order (top-to-bottom) may follow information-structural principles rather than fixed syntax. The 100% ordinal/position match suggests order is meaningful but its logic is not yet determined.

## 10. What Quechua Lacks

These gaps **predict** what khipu will not have:

| Absent feature | Detail | Khipu prediction |
|---|---|---|
| **Articles** | No "the"/"a" | No article-like markers expected |
| **Gender** | No nominal or verbal gender; pronouns undifferentiated | No gender encoding expected |
| **Obligatory plural** | *-kuna* is optional; plurality often unmarked | Plurality may be unmarked or contextual |
| **Relative pronouns** | Relativization via nominalization (§3) | No "which"-type markers expected |
| **Prosodic focus** | Focus is morphological (§5) | Visual/morphological focus expected, not positional stress |
| **Copula (present)** | *Kay* ("to be") often omitted in present | Equational statements may be juxtaposition |
