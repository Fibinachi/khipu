# Colonial Grammar Notes: Quechua as Described 1560–1607

**Purpose:** Extract grammatical descriptions from the two earliest Quechua grammars for khipu decipherment reference. Priority: what they describe that modern Quechua has lost, and how their analysis distorts (or reveals) the categories relevant to khipu structure.

**Sources:**
- Santo Tomás, Domingo de. *Grammatica, o Arte de la lengua general de los Indios de los Reynos del Peru.* Valladolid, 1560. (`grammaticaoarted00domi` on archive.org)
- González Holguín, Diego. *Gramatica y arte nueva de la lengua general de todo el Peru.* Lima, 1607. (1842 reprint digitized as `gramticayartenue00gonz` on archive.org)

**Method caveat:** Both are OCR'd 16th/17th-century Spanish typography. Quotations below are transcribed from the OCR with obvious errors silently corrected where the reading is unambiguous. Page/section references are to the digitized editions.

## 1. Evidential particles: what they saw and what they missed

### Holguín on =mi (Libro II, cap. on particles)

Holguín describes =mi at length but **entirely misses the evidential dimension**:

> "Esta partícula *na*, o *mi* se pone siempre en todos los verbos y todos los tiempos del modo indicativo porqué el indicativo todo es afirmativo, y la *m*, o *mi* es señal de verbo afirmativo, y como [la] partícula *chu* es señal de interrogativos y siempre se pone en habiendo interrogación así siempre se pone *m*, o *mi* con verbo afirmativo."

Translation: "This particle *-n* or *=mi* is always placed on all verbs and all tenses of the indicative mood, because the indicative is entirely affirmative, and the *-m* or *=mi* is the sign of the affirmative verb; and as the particle *-chu* is the sign of interrogatives and is always placed when there is interrogation, so always is *-m* or *=mi* placed with the affirmative verb."

He further notes:
- =mi is **not** placed when the verb is interrogative or dubitative: *"sino es cuando el verbo de indicativo es interrogativo, o dubitativo, con ychae, o en, o cha, o chu, que entonces no afirma"* — except with *-cha* (dubitative) or *-chu* (interrogative/negative), which "then does not affirm."
- =mi is **not placed on the verb itself** when avoidable: *"Nunca se pone en el mismo verbo de indicativo sino es cuando no hay antes otra partícula en que se pueda poner, y si la hay se pone en la persona que hace o en la que padece, o en el adverbio"* — it's placed on the agent, the patient, or the adverb.
- Phonological allomorphy: *"-m* for [words] ending in a vowel, *mi* for [words ending in] a consonant or two vowels."

### Analysis

| Colonial description | Modern analysis | Khipu implication |
|---|---|---|
| =mi = "affirmative" marker | =mi = direct/best-grounds evidential (Faller 2002) | Holguín saw the distribution (obligatory on indicatives) but mislabeled the function. The *obligatoriness* is what matters for khipu: if =mi is required on every indicative clause, its khipu equivalent (color-marking?) should be similarly systematic. |
| -chu = "interrogative" | -chu = interrogative/negative | Correct as far as it goes. |
| -cha = "dubitative" | =chá = conjectural evidential | Partially correct — he caught the epistemic uncertainty but not the evidential (non-witnessed) component. |
| =mi placement on agent/patient/adverb | Second-position clitic with focus function | **Directly relevant:** if the evidential marker attaches to the focused constituent (not the verb), then in khipu the "evidential" color-marking should appear on the *noun* (the cord), not on a verbal element. This is consistent with our finding that color-marking is a cord-level property. |
| No mention of =si (reportative) as a category | =si = reportative evidential | **Significant gap.** If Holguín doesn't describe =si, either it was less prominent in 1607 Cuzco Quechua, or he didn't recognize it. For khipu: the reportative channel may have a different structural realization than the =mi channel. |

### The Latin-category distortion

Holguín forces =mi into the Latin indicative/subjunctive distinction. He cannot conceive of a grammatical category that marks *information source* because Latin lacks one. His "affirmative" label is a Procrustean fit: =mi does correlate with affirmative (non-interrogative, non-dubitative) clauses, but the *reason* is evidential, not illocutionary.

**For khipu work:** prefer Holguín's *distributional* observations (where =mi goes, when it's blocked) over his *functional* labels (what he says it means). The distribution is data; the label is theory.

## 2. Verb paradigms: tense and aspect

### Holguín's eight moods

Holguín imposes the full Latin system: *"todas las partes de cualquier verbo son ocho; indicativo, imperativo, optativo, subjuntivo, infinitivo, participios, gerundios, supinos"* — all verbs have eight parts: indicative, imperative, optative, subjunctive, infinitive, participles, gerunds, supines.

This is pure Latin imposition. Quechua does not have these categories. However, the *paradigms he provides* under these labels contain real data:

- **"Pretérito imperfecto"** (*-rqa-*): *"Cachcarcani, yo era o estaba"* — the witnessed past.
- **"Pretérito perfecto"** forms with *-sqa-*: *"Munascca casccaymi, yo he sido amado"* — the participial/narrative past.
- **"Plusquamperfecto"**: *"Manascca casccavmicarca, yo habia sido amado."*

### The -rqa / -sqa distinction

Holguín provides both paradigms but does **not** identify the evidential contrast between them. He treats *-sqa-* forms as participial/perfect constructions (which they morphologically are) without noting that *-sqa* past vs *-rqa* past encodes witnessed vs non-witnessed.

| Colonial description | Modern analysis | Khipu implication |
|---|---|---|
| -rqa- = "pretérito imperfecto" | -rqa = witnessed past | — |
| -sqa- = participial perfect | -sqa = narrative/non-witnessed past | **If khipu encode tense at all**, the witnessed/non-witnessed past distinction is the most likely candidate (it's the most grammatically obligatory). But our Conklin test suggests khipu use *constructional chronology* (depth = later) instead of morphological tense. The colonial grammars confirm that 1607 Quechua *had* the morphological tense system — so its absence in khipu (if confirmed) is a deliberate structural choice, not a gap in the language. |

### Santo Tomás on verb "transitions"

Santo Tomás (1560) devotes a full chapter (*"Cap. sexto de algunas maneras de hablar particulares que ay por el verbo y conjugación del"*) to what he calls the *"transiciones"* — the bipersonal subject→object agreement system. He gives rules like:

> *"Todas las vezes que el acto del verbo passa de la primera persona a la segunda..."* — Whenever the action of the verb passes from first person to second...

This is the famous Quechua 4×3 person-marking system (1→2, 1→3, 2→1, 2→3, 3→1, 3→2, 3→3, etc.), each with distinct suffixes.

| Colonial description | Modern analysis | Khipu implication |
|---|---|---|
| "Transiciones" — verb action "passes" between persons | Bipersonal agreement (subject and object marked on verb) | **If khipu encoded verbal agreement**, we would expect a similarly complex person-marking system. Its absence supports the "no verbs" hypothesis: khipu don't need person agreement because they don't have verbs. The *social* relationship (who gives to whom) is instead encoded in the *hierarchy* (parent→subsidiary) and *color contrast*. |

## 3. Noun cases and postpositions

### Holguín's head-final principle

Holguín states an explicit, general word-order rule:

> *"Hayla muy cierta e inviolable, y es, que de cualquier manera que un caso es regido de otro siempre él que es regido se antepone al que le rige."*

Translation: "There is [a rule] very certain and inviolable, which is that in whatever way one case is governed by another, the governed always precedes the governor."

He gives examples: oblique cases (genitive through ablative) precede the nominative; a governed nominative precedes its governor.

| Colonial description | Modern analysis | Khipu implication |
|---|---|---|
| Governed-before-governor (head-final) | SOV, postpositions, modifier-before-head | **Directly confirms** the structural principle behind khipu layout: subsidiaries (governed) hang from parents (governors); modifiers precede heads. The khipu's spatial syntax *is* Quechua's head-final syntax realized topologically rather than linearly. This is the strongest colonial confirmation of the "syntax is the layout" hypothesis. |

## 4. Word order: SOV

Holguín does not state "SOV" in modern terms (the concept postdates him), but the head-final principle above, combined with his paradigms (which consistently show subject-object-verb order in examples), confirms SOV as the unmarked order.

Santo Tomás's examples similarly show verb-final order throughout.

| Colonial description | Modern analysis | Khipu implication |
|---|---|---|
| Governed-precedes-governor; verb-final in all paradigms | SOV canonical, all 6 orders grammatical (Cerrón-Palomino 1994) | For khipu: the "verb" (document type) comes last — which is consistent with the file-type hypothesis where the document format *is* the predicate. You don't read the "verb" until you've processed the entire khipu. |

## 5. Archaic features: what 1607 had that modern Quechua lost

This is the most khipu-relevant section. Features present in the colonial grammars but absent or reduced in modern Quechua are candidates for features present in khipu (which are *older* than the colonial grammars).

### Candidate archaisms (requiring further verification)

1. **Robust =mi obligatoriness.** Holguín describes =mi as required on *every* indicative verb. Modern Cuzco Quechua still has =mi, but its obligatoriness may be reduced in some registers. If khipu color-marking is more systematic than modern =mi usage, the colonial description is the better baseline.

2. **The inclusive/exclusive "we" in verbal agreement.** Both grammars describe *-nchik* (inclusive) vs *-yku* (exclusive) in detail. This distinction is robust in modern Quechua but its *social* function (who counts as "us") may have been more grammatically elaborated in 1607. For khipu: the ayllu/community as "we" vs outsiders may map to the inclusive/exclusive distinction in color-marking.

3. **Santo Tomás's coastal variety.** Santo Tomás explicitly describes a *coastal* variant of "Classical Quechua" (learned in the Chancay Valley). This is distinct from Holguín's highland Cuzco variety. **This is directly relevant to the dialect hypothesis:** coastal khipu (Inkawasi, Pachacamac, Cajamarquilla) may reflect the coastal variety Santo Tomás describes, not Holguín's Cuzco norm. The two grammars together provide a highland/coastal comparison baseline.

4. **Verbal "supines" and "gerunds."** Holguín's Latin labels notwithstanding, the *forms* he paradigms under these headings (various nominalized verb forms) may include constructions that have since been lost or restructured. These require specialist review.

### What the grammars do NOT describe (significant absences)

- **No description of =si (reportative) as a distinct category.** Either it was less salient in 1607, or the grammarians missed it. Modern Faller (2002) makes it central.
- **No recognition of evidentiality as a system.** The entire category is invisible to both grammarians. This means colonial Quechua *had* evidentiality (it's inherited, not innovated) but Spanish observers couldn't see it — which is a cautionary tale for khipu: the most grammatically central features may be the hardest for outsiders to recognize.

## The Latin-category distortion: a field guide

Where the colonial analysis seems forced, prefer the raw paradigms over the interpretation:

| Forced category | What's actually happening | How to use the data |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Latin moods | Quechua has 2-3 morphological moods + nominalizations | Ignore mood labels; use the suffix paradigms |
| =mi as "affirmative" | =mi as evidential | Use distribution (where it appears, where blocked); discard "affirmative" label |
| "Pretérito imperfecto/perfecto/plusquamperfecto" | -rqa- vs -sqa- aspectual/evidential pasts | Use the forms; the Latin tense labels mislead |
| "Supinos" and "gerundios" | Various nominalized/subordinate verb forms | The forms are real; the Latin function labels are not |
| "Transiciones" | Bipersonal agreement | The term is actually apt; the paradigms are reliable |

**Rule of thumb:** When Holguín or Santo Tomás *describe where something goes*, trust them. When they *explain what it means* using Latin categories, verify against modern analysis.
