# Cañete Valley Late Horizon Population Estimate

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Purpose:** Test whether Inkawasi quipu white-cord numbers (15,031; 13,162; 12,600) could represent district population figures.

## Settlement Data

Source: Hill (2020), *Inca Strategies of Conquest and Control*, UCLA dissertation. Chapter 4 archaeological reconnaissance of the Cañete middle valley (Lunahuaná), with lower valley data from prior research (Marcus 1987, etc.).

### Middle Valley Inca Sites (with measured areas)

| Site | Size | Elevation | Zone | Character |
|------|------|-----------|------|-----------|
| Cerro Suero | 8 ha | 553 masl | 2 | Large settlement, plazas, domestic terraces, cemetery |
| Cantagallo | 6 ha | 582 masl | 2 | Large site, planned Inca compound, terracing |
| Cruz Blanca | 6 ha | 865 masl | 1 | Large Late Horizon site, colonnades, plazas |
| Huajil | 3 ha | 747 masl | 1 | Site complex, planned Inca architecture, plaza |
| **Total** | **23 ha** | | | |

Additional middle valley sites mentioned without hectare figures: Yapana Huancapuquio (small settlement), Quebrada Concón complex, La Toma (Inca settlement near lower end of Lunahuaná).

Inkawasi (the storehouse itself) is described as "the largest Inca installation in Cañete" but is primarily military/storage, not residential. Excluded from residential area totals.

### Lower Valley

No hectare figures available in Hill (2020) for lower valley residential sites. Key lower valley sites (Huarco/Cerro Azul, Cancharí, Vilcahuasi, Ungará) are described qualitatively. Marcus (1987) documents "extensive domestic terracing" at Huarco/Cerro Azul but provides no area measurement in the cited text.

**This is the binding data gap.** The lower valley was the demographic core (Huarco polity), and we cannot quantify it from available sources.

## Population Calculation

### Middle Valley Only

Standard Andean coastal density coefficients:

| Density | 23 ha × coefficient | Population |
|---------|---------------------|------------|
| 100 persons/ha (conservative) | 23 × 100 | **2,300** |
| 150 persons/ha (moderate) | 23 × 150 | **3,450** |
| 200 persons/ha (dense urban) | 23 × 200 | **4,600** |

### Comparison with Quipu Numbers

| Quipu figure | vs. 2,300 (low) | vs. 3,450 (mid) | vs. 4,600 (high) |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 15,031 | 6.5× | 4.4× | 3.3× |
| 13,162 | 5.7× | 3.8× | 2.9× |
| 12,600 | 5.5× | 3.7× | 2.7× |

## Interpretation

The quipu figures (12,600–15,031) are **3–6× larger** than the middle valley residential estimate (2,300–4,600).

**Three possible readings:**

1. **The quipu record the whole valley, not just the middle.** If the lower valley (Huarco polity core) held 2–3× the middle valley population, total valley population could reach 7,000–14,000 — bringing the quipu numbers into plausible range. This is consistent with ethnohistoric accounts of Huarco as the dominant pre-Inca polity.

2. **The quipu numbers are commodity totals, not population.** Bulk agricultural figures (bushels of maize, etc.) in the five-figure range are expected for a state storehouse. The white-cord-equals-people hypothesis would then apply only to small-number contexts.

3. **Both.** White cords might encode people at small scales (household census: 1–4) and commodities at large scales (storehouse inventory: 12,000+). The color alone doesn't disambiguate — context (site type, associated values) does.

## Assumptions and Limitations

1. **Density coefficients** (100–200/ha) are standard for Andean coastal settlements but not calibrated to Cañete specifically. Actual density depends on architecture type, household size, and site function.

2. **Contemporaneity** is assumed but not proven. The four measured sites have Late Horizon components, but may not all have been fully occupied simultaneously. Some may have peaked at different times within the 1450–1532 window.

3. **Survey completeness.** Hill's survey covered the middle valley (200–900 masl). Sites may be missed due to modern agriculture, looting, or burial under alluvium. The 23 ha is a floor, not a ceiling.

4. **Lower valley data gap** is the critical limitation. Without hectare figures for Huarco/Cerro Azul, Cancharí, Vilcahuasi, and other lower valley settlements, valley-wide population cannot be estimated from archaeology alone.

5. **Inkawasi's catchment** is unknown. We don't know whether the storehouse served the middle valley only, the whole Cañete Valley, or a multi-valley district. A larger catchment makes the population hypothesis more plausible.

## What's Needed

- Hectare measurements for lower valley Late Horizon residential sites (Huarco/Cerro Azul, Cancharí, Vilcahuasi)
- Any colonial census or tribute list for the Cañete Valley (cf. the Santa Valley 1670 census used by Medrano & Urton)
- The Inkawasi quipu-to-commodity mapping from Chu's excavation records (which quipu was found with which product)

## Bottom Line

The middle valley alone (2,300–4,600 people) is too small to match the quipu figures (12,600–15,031). But the lower valley — the demographic core — is unquantified. If it held 2–3× the middle valley population, the numbers align. **The population hypothesis is neither confirmed nor falsified by available archaeological data.** The lower valley survey data is the missing piece.
