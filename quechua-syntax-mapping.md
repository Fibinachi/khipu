# Anomalous Khipu Features → Quechua Syntax Mapping

Version 1.0 — 2026-10-11.
Premise: Quechua is living; its grammar should map onto khipu structure because the makers thought in Quechua.
Scope: 20 anomalous khipu (see anomalous-transcriptions.md, transcription-notation.md).

**Confidence tiers used throughout:**
- **STRONG** — structural parallel directly observable; specific construction inferred but the shape matches.
- **SUGGESTIVE** — plausible parallel with supporting logic; alternative readings exist.
- **SPECULATIVE** — thin evidence; offered as hypothesis, not finding.
- **NO PARALLEL** — feature is likely non-grammatical (repair, material, data quality); stated explicitly.

**Already established (prior work, not re-argued here):** color-marking intensity tracks evidential distance (subsidiaries 3.26x more likely marked than mains, p=1.5e-213), paralleling Quechua's obligatory evidentiality (-mi/-si/-cha). That mapping is STRONG and grounds the rest.

## 1. Phantom positions in fixed color patterns (1000144) — STRONG

**Feature:** 6-pendant groups with a fixed color sequence (LB, BS-W, B-W, G0/BS, MB/W, 0D/W); where groups have 6 pendants the 6th position is "non-existent," where 5, both 6th and 7th. Empty slots in a rigid template.

**Quechua parallel:** Templatic morphology with zero exponence. Quechua nominal and verbal suffixes occupy fixed ordered slots (cf. the 7-slot nominal template: possessor, number, derivation ×2, case ×3 — Ríos 2015). Not every slot fills in every form; the empty slot is a **zero morpheme**, not missing data. The paradigm defines the positions; individual forms leave some empty.

**Why plausible:** The structure is identical — a fixed positional template where absence is meaningful because the template predicts presence. The investigators' language ("non-existent position") already treats the slot as real. In Quechua morphology, you would never say a word "lacks" a case suffix; it bears zero case (nominative). Same logic.

**Confirm:** Find the same phantom-slot pattern in non-anomalous khipu of the same color-group family. If the template recurs with the same empty positions, it is paradigmatic.
**Refute:** If phantom positions are randomly distributed (no recurring template), they are data loss, not zero morphemes.

## 2. Recursive pendant-through-subsidiary-knot (1000178) — SUGGESTIVE

**Feature:** Pendant 6 is slipped *through a knot on its own subsidiary*. A cord passes through a knot tied on its own dependent — structural self-embedding.

**Quechua parallel:** Nominalized relative clause embedding. Quechua builds recursion via nominalizers (-na, -sqa, -q): a full clause is nominalized and embedded as an argument inside another clause ("the [going-NMLZ] I want" = "I want you to go"). The embedded clause refers back to an element of the matrix clause — self-reference across levels.

**Why plausible:** Self-embedding is rare in physical cordage and hard to produce accidentally. The pendant literally re-enters its own dependency subtree, which is what a relative clause does syntactically: the head noun appears inside the clause that modifies it. Quechua relative clauses are formed exactly this way (nominalized clause + head).

**Alternative:** A repair (re-attachment through available structure). The knot on the subsidiary may have been a convenient anchor, not a grammatical device.
**Confirm:** Find a second instance. One occurrence is an anecdote; two is a construction.
**Refute:** If the through-knot shows repair-thread or mending flags, it is mechanical, not syntactic.

## 3. Pendant-to-pendant binding (1000344) — SUGGESTIVE

**Feature:** P10's finishing knot is tied *around P11* — the end of one pendant fastened to its neighbor, 5.5cm from P11's bottom.

**Quechua parallel:** Switch-reference marking (-shpa SS / -pti DS). Quechua obligatorily marks whether adjacent chained clauses share a subject (same-subject -shpa, subject unexpressed) or differ (different-subject -pti, subject cross-referenced). A physical link between adjacent pendants marks their relationship: linked = same source/agent, unlinked = independent.

**Why plausible:** The linkage is between *adjacent* pendants, matching switch-reference's clause-adjacency domain. The project already established that subsidiaries mark source (evidentiality mapping); pendant-to-pendant linkage extends source-tracking laterally. SS clauses in Quechua are structurally *reduced* (no subject agreement) — the linked pendant is likewise structurally dependent on its neighbor.

**Alternative:** Simple coordination ("and") — P10 and P11 belong together, no subject semantics.
**Confirm:** Check whether linked pendant pairs share color (same source → SS reading) more often than unlinked adjacent pairs.
**Refute:** If linked pairs systematically *differ* in color/source, the SS reading fails; coordination or error-correction remains.

## 4. Multi-khipu joins (1000008, 1000005) — SUGGESTIVE

**Feature:** 1000008: one primary-cord knot unites three khipu (257D + 257C + 257F). 1000005: two khipu with different main-cord thicknesses (0.3cm vs 0.5cm) joined.

**Quechua parallel:** Clause chaining in narrative discourse. Quechua chains multiple dependent clauses (-shpa/-pti) into long narrative sequences under a single finite verb. The chain is a discourse unit larger than the clause. Joining physical documents = joining discourse units; the uniting knot is a discourse connective.

**Why plausible:** The join is at the *primary cord* (the document root), not at pendant level — this is document-level syntax, not cord-level. Different cord thicknesses in 1000005 show the joined pieces were made separately (different hands/batches) then united, exactly like chained clauses composed separately then linked by switch-reference morphology.

**Confirm:** Check whether joined khipu share color palette or value ranges (same administrative context → same "narrative"). Check switch-reference correlate: do the joined pieces show the pendant-to-pendant linkage of §3 at the junction?
**Refute:** If joins are always accompanied by repair/mending flags, they are archival (museum) joins, not Inca discourse structure.

## 5. Marker cords tied through knots (1000183) — SUGGESTIVE

**Feature:** Short 2cm cords (YB:0G or W) tied *through* knots at the bottom, on specific pendants (P1, 2, 4, 65, 67, 85–88, ...). Not at group boundaries — through individual knots.

**Quechua parallel:** Focus marking. In Quechua, the evidential enclitic attaches to the *focused/asserted* constituent (Faller 2002; Mannheim: "evidential suffixes mark the sentence constituent that is making the actual assertion"). Focus is also marked by -m (emphatic). A physical flag through a specific knot says "this value is the asserted one" — it marks informational prominence, not structural position.

**Why plausible:** The markers target knots (values), not cord boundaries — they annotate content, matching focus (which highlights a constituent's content) rather than topic -qa (which marks constituent edges). The two color variants (YB:0G vs W) may distinguish focus types, paralleling -m (emphatic) vs -mi (assertive focus).

**Confirm:** Test whether flagged knots carry unusual values (outliers, round numbers, corrections) vs unflagged knots on the same khipu. Focus should correlate with informational salience.
**Refute:** If flagged positions are random with respect to value, they may be tallying aids or post-hoc annotations, not grammatical focus.

## 6. Signal threads / señales (1000004, 1000005) — SUGGESTIVE

**Feature:** Red (or white) threads woven *into* pendant fibers at measured distances (26cm, 32cm, 34.5cm); a loose pendant tied above a long knot.

**Quechua parallel:** Discourse enclitics (-qa topic, -m emphatic, -puni, -raq). These attach to constituents without changing propositional content — they manage information structure (what's topical, what's emphasized, what's new). A signal thread adds meta-information to a pendant without altering its knots.

**Why plausible:** Like enclitics, signal threads are *secondary* attachments (woven in, not structural), positioned *within* the constituent (at measured distances along the pendant, paralleling enclitic placement inside the phrase), and color-coded (red vs white ≈ different enclitic functions).

**Distinguish from §5:** Marker cords (§5) go through *knots* (focus on value); signal threads go through *fibers* (discourse status of the cord). This matches the Quechua division between focus evidentials (on the asserted constituent) and topic enclitics (on the topical constituent).
**Confirm:** Check whether signaled pendants cluster at group boundaries (topic → -qa) vs mid-group (focus → -m).
**Refute:** If signal colors correlate with pendant color rather than position, they may be decorative or batch markers.

## 7. U-direction / reversed-twist knots — SUGGESTIVE (repair reading primary)

**Feature:** U-direction (reversed) knots cluster on anomalous cords. Pendant 70 of 1000201 was replaced upside-down: all knots U-direction AND knot order reversed (6s,5L,E instead of 1s,5L,6s), flagged [rev][mended].

**Quechua parallel (primary):** Repair / correction marking. The 1000201 case is explicitly a *replacement* — the reversed direction marks "this cord is not original." Quechua has no dedicated repair morpheme, but discourse repairs are flagged prosodically and lexically. The physical reversal is the written equivalent: a meta-signal that the normal reading is suspended.

**Quechua parallel (alternative):** Evidential contrast. If normal (Z-twist) = -mi (direct/witnessed), reversed (U-twist) could = -cha (inferred) or -si (reported) — a binary twist opposition encoding a binary evidential opposition. The clustering of U-knots on *anomalous* cords fits: anomalous = less certain = non-direct evidential.

**Why the repair reading leads:** The clearest case (1000201) co-occurs with a mending flag and a physically replaced pendant. Context beats system.
**Confirm (repair):** Check whether all U-direction clusters co-occur with mending/replacement evidence. If yes, U = "repaired."
**Confirm (evidential):** Find U-direction knots on un-mended cords in positions where reported (vs witnessed) knowledge is expected — e.g., on rider subsidiaries. If U marks subsidiaries systematically, the evidential reading gains.
**Refute both:** If U-direction correlates with fiber type or spinning direction of the source thread, it is material, not meaningful.

## 8. Terminal lone E on 1000522 — SUGGESTIVE

**Feature:** The tufted-tally khipu (128 TF knots, flat, unary) ends with a single figure-eight knot alone on cord 95, the last cord.

**Quechua parallel:** Clause-final enclitic position. Quechua evidentials and validators (-mi, -si, -chu) are *enclitics* — they attach at the right edge of the clause, after all other morphology. The lone E sits at the right edge of the document, after all tally content. Positional parallel: edge-marking.

**Why plausible:** In the standard decimal system E=1, but this khipu has no decimal structure — the E cannot be read as a digit here. Its isolation and terminal position are its meaning, just as a Quechua enclitic's clause-final position is part of its function. If TF tallies are the "proposition," the terminal E is the "illocutionary force" — cf. Faller's analysis of evidentials as illocutionary operators.

**Confirm:** Find other unary/non-decimal khipu with terminal singleton knots. Check whether the terminal knot type varies (E vs other) and whether variation correlates with anything.
**Refute:** If the E is on a cord that also bears TF knots in other specimens, it is part of the tally, not an edge marker.

## 9. Tufted unary tally system (1000522) — SPECULATIVE

**Feature:** 128 tufted knots across 83 flat cords (1–4 per cord), no S/L/E decimal structure. Each tuft = 1 (unary). Plus a carrying sling.

**Quechua parallel (tentative):** Register difference — the distinction between fully inflected language and reduced/elliptical speech. Quechua allows radical ellipsis (arguments dropped, minimal morphology) in context-rich settings. A unary tally is counting *without* the decimal inflectional machinery — like gesturing "one, one, one" instead of saying "a hundred twenty-eight." It may be a field tally: rapid, portable (hence the sling), later to be transferred into a decimal khipu.

**Why speculative:** There is no direct Quechua morpheme for "unary counting." The parallel is functional (reduced code for rapid use), not structural. The sling supports portability, but portability doesn't prove a register distinction.

**Confirm:** Find the "transfer" — a decimal khipu whose values sum to ~128 associated with the same context/provenance (Ica/Pisco). A field-tally → office-ledger pipeline would confirm the register reading.
**Refute:** If tufted tallies appear in archival/final contexts (not field), the rapid-tally hypothesis fails.

## 10. Spiral knots (1000504 + 9 others) — SPECULATIVE

**Feature:** 130 spiral knots (SP) on 1000504, with axis/no-axis distinction (SP{U} vs SP{AXU}); spirals on 9 other quipu including 4 Inkawasi warehouse pieces. Spirals appear *within* normal hierarchical structures, not replacing them.

**Quechua parallel (tentative):** Derivational vs inflectional morphology. Quechua verbs carry inflectional suffixes (person, tense, evidential — closed, obligatory, regular: like S/L/E decimal knots) AND derivational suffixes (causative -chi, desiderative -naya, etc. — open, lexical, optional: like spirals adding non-numerical content). Spirals may encode *what kind* of thing is counted (derivational/lexical) while S/L/E encode *how many* (inflectional/grammatical).

**Why speculative:** We have no reading of any spiral. The distributional fact (embedded within normal structures, including administrative Inkawasi) supports "additional channel," but the derivational parallel is inferred from that distribution alone.

**Confirm:** Test whether spiral-bearing cords share color or position patterns (e.g., all on subsidiaries of a certain color → derivational category). The axis/no-axis distinction (U vs AXU) may encode a binary derivational contrast — test what it correlates with.
**Refute:** If spirals are randomly distributed with respect to color, level, and value, they may be idiosyncratic or maker-specific, not grammatical.

## 11. Subsidiaries through doubled pendant end (1000239) — SPECULATIVE

**Feature:** Pendant 21 has 6 subsidiaries attached *through its doubled (folded) end*.

**Quechua parallel (tentative):** Comitative/inclusive coordination (-wan "with," -ntin "inclusive"). Multiple subsidiaries sharing one attachment point = coordinated dependents under a single head, like Quechua conjoined NPs sharing one case marker. The doubled end creates a physical "loop" that holds the group — the cord-level equivalent of a single case suffix scoping over a coordinated phrase.

**Why thin:** Doubled ends may be purely mechanical (stronger attachment for heavy subsidiary loads). Six subsidiaries is a heavy load; doubling the end distributes weight.

**Confirm:** Check whether doubled-end pendants systematically carry more subsidiaries than single-end pendants. If doubling correlates with subsidiary count *beyond* what weight alone predicts (e.g., doubled ends with few subsidiaries), the grammatical reading gains.
**Refute:** If doubled ends correlate only with heavy subsidiary loads, it is engineering.

## 12. No-axis knots, B/3453 Cajamarquilla series — NO PARALLEL

**Feature:** 5 quipu (1000310, 1000486, 1000489, 1000491, 1000651) whose knots lack the spin/ply axis defining S/L/E; the investigator resorted to size-grading (A/B/C).

**Assessment:** No plausible Quechua grammatical parallel. Axis is a *fiber* property (spin direction of the plied cord), not a morphological choice. Its absence most likely reflects material/technological difference (different fiber preparation, degradation, or a regional cord-making tradition), not a syntactic distinction. Quechua grammar has no "axis" equivalent because axis is phonetic (like voicing), not morphemic — and even phonetically, its absence would be a dialect/accent feature, not a syntactic one.

**What to do instead:** Fiber analysis. If the B/3453 cords use different fiber or spin than standard khipu, the "different language" is technological. This is a materials question, not a grammar question.

## 13. Z-spiral roll construction — NO PARALLEL (orthographic)

**Feature:** Khipu rolls right-to-left, pendants facing away (Z-spiral bundle).

**Assessment:** This is orthographic convention (reading direction), not syntax — parallel to "Quechua is written left-to-right in Latin script," which tells you nothing about Quechua grammar. Noted for completeness; no syntactic mapping proposed.

## 14. Knots bunched at top, place value unreadable — NO PARALLEL (data quality)

**Feature:** All knots near the principal cord; tens/hundreds indistinguishable; direction unclear.

**Assessment:** Recording limitation or degradation, not a grammatical feature. Excluded from syntactic mapping.

## Methodological note

The strongest mappings (§1, plus the previously established evidentiality→color-marking) share a property: they are **obligatory, systematic, and paradigmatically constrained** — exactly the properties that define grammar as opposed to usage. The speculative mappings (§9–11) lack one or more of these properties. This is the criterion going forward: a khipu feature earns a Quechua syntactic parallel only if it behaves like grammar (obligatory within its domain, systematically distributed, paradigmatically opposed to alternatives).
