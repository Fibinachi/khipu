# Lower Cañete Valley: Late Horizon Site Areas and Population Estimates

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Purpose:** Test whether Inkawasi quipu white-cord figures (15,031; 13,162; 12,600) could represent district population.

## Site Area Data

### Lower Valley (yunga) — Major Late Horizon Sites

Source: Marcone Flores & Areche Espinola (2015), *Cuadernos del Qhapaq Ñan* 3(3):48-68, citing Williams & Merino (1974) archaeological cadastre.

| Site | Area (ha) | Character |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Vilcahuasi (Huacones-Vilcahuasi) | 80 | Largest complex; probable Huarco capital/power center; Inca additions |
| Ungará (Fortaleza de Ungará) | 40 | Hilltop fortress; canal intake control |
| El Huarco-Cerro Azul | 32 | Intermediate; primarily religious-ritual under Inca (not population center) |
| Cancharí (Fortaleza de Cancharí) | 2 | Elite palace; small but architecturally complex |

**Total major sites:** 154 ha (total site extent, not residential-only)

**Additional context:**
- 32 late-period sites recorded in lower valley (Williams & Merino 1974)
- ~55% are commoner settlements (non-elite residential)
- Site sizes did not change substantially between Late Intermediate Period and Late Horizon (Inca conquest did not transform settlement pattern in lower valley)

**Note discrepancy:** Hill (2020) lists Cruz Blanca residential area as 6 ha vs 60 ha total site extent here. Total site area includes ceremonial, defensive, agricultural, and empty space — residential is a fraction.

### Middle Valley Residential (Hill 2020)

From Hill's dissertation survey:
- Cerro Suero: 8 ha
- Cantagallo: 6 ha

- **Total: 23 ha residential**

## Population Estimates

### Method
Standard Andean coastal density coefficients: 100 / 150 / 200 persons per residential hectare.

### Lower Valley

**Problem:** We have total site extents (154 ha for 4 major sites), not residential-only areas. The residential fraction is unknown.

**Scenario A — If 50% of major site area is residential (77 ha):**
- At 100/ha: 7,700 people
- At 150/ha: 11,550 people
- At 200/ha: 15,400 people

**Scenario B — If 30% residential (46 ha):**
- At 100/ha: 4,600 people
- At 150/ha: 6,930 people
- At 200/ha: 9,240 people

**Plus commoner settlements:** ~17 additional sites (55% of 32). If average 2 ha residential each = 34 ha → 3,400–6,800 additional people.

**Lower valley total range: roughly 8,000–22,000** depending on assumptions.

### Comparison with Inkawasi Quipu Figures

| Quipu figure | Lower valley pop. range | In range? |
|--------------|------------------------|-----------|
| 15,031 | 8,000–22,000 | **Yes** (upper-middle) |
| 13,162 | 8,000–22,000 | **Yes** (middle) |
| 12,600 | 8,000–22,000 | **Yes** (middle) |

### Critical Caveats

1. **Inkawasi is in the middle valley, not the lower valley.** If the quipu record Inkawasi's administrative catchment, it may cover the chaupiyunga/Lunahuaná señorío, not the lower valley/Huarco señorío. The two were separate polities before Inca conquest.

2. **Middle valley residential (23 ha) gives only 2,300–4,600 people** — well below the quipu figures. If Inkawasi administered only the middle valley, the population hypothesis fails.

3. **Residential fraction is a guess.** No publication separates residential from total site area for these lower valley sites. The 50% and 30% scenarios are illustrative, not evidence-based.

4. **Cerro Azul (32 ha) was primarily ritual**, not residential. Including it inflates the estimate.

5. **Contemporaneity unproven.** The 80 ha Vilcahuasi figure may reflect maximum extent across periods, not simultaneous Late Horizon occupation.

6. **The quipu figures could be commodity totals**, not population. Bulk goods (15,031 units of beans?) remain plausible.

## What Would Resolve This

1. **Residential-only area measurements** for Vilcahuasi, Ungará, Cancharí, and lower valley commoner sites. Williams & Merino (1974) 2-volume inventory may have sector-level breakdowns — not digitized.

2. **Colonial census or tribute list for Cañete** analogous to the Santa Valley 1670 census. Would give actual population figures to compare.

3. **Chu's Inkawasi excavation records** mapping quipu to findspot commodities. If the white-cord quipu were found with food remains (not in residential contexts), commodity interpretation strengthens.

4. **Clarify Inkawasi's administrative catchment.** Did it administer the whole valley or just Lunahuaná/chaupiyunga?

## Bottom Line

Lower valley Late Horizon site areas are now documented: Vilcahuasi (80 ha), Ungará (40 ha), Huarco-Cerro Azul (32 ha), Cancharí (2 ha). Population estimates derived from these (8,000–22,000) bracket the Inkawasi quipu figures (12,600–15,031), but the range is too wide and the assumptions too many for confirmation. The hypothesis that white-cord large numbers = district population is **plausible but unproven**. The binding constraints are: (a) residential vs total area breakdown unknown, (b) Inkawasi's catchment area undefined, (c) no colonial census for Cañete to anchor against.
