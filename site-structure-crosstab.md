## 4. The Checklist Hypothesis — VERDICT: Widespread type, not local practice

**Operational definition:** >50% cords values 0–3 AND >30% zero-value AND <5 distinct colors AND ≥5 decoded cords.

**Result: 36 / 250 khipu (14.4%) meet the criteria.**

Site distribution:
| Site | n |
|---|---:|
| unknown | 15 |
| Pachacamac | 8 |
| Cajamarquilla | 2 |
| Pueblo Libre, Lima | 2 |
| Pisco, Quillagua, Peru, Callango, Incahuasi, Armatambo, Ica-Pisco, Central Coast, Ica | 1 each |

**Checklist-like khipu are scattered across 13 provenances.** They do not cluster at 1–2 sites. This is a widespread document type (or a widespread structural mode), not a Pachacamac-local practice.

Notable: the most extreme cases (100% categorical, 100% zero) include 1000056 (Pachacamac, 42 cords), 1000489 (Cajamarquilla, 18 cords, 1 color), 1000537 (Pisco, 24 cords). These are not damaged quantitative khipu — the systematic zeros across all cords of a single color argue for intentional categorical recording.

## 5. Site Fingerprints

Corpus medians for reference: cat%=63 zero%=34 gram%=70 max=191 maxlvl=2 ncolors=5

### Pachacamac (n=52) — the quantitative administrator
- cat% 41 (below corpus) · max 715 (3.7× corpus) · gram% 76 (above corpus)
- 98% Z-twist. Mixed clusters (28 sparse / 24 flat).
- **Fingerprint:** large values, grammatical-tier dominant, standard Z-twist. The most "normal" administrative site — closest to the census/warehouse norms.

### Incahuasi (n=20) — the categorical warehouse
- cat% **90** (1.4× corpus) · zero% **71** (2.1× corpus) · gram% 56 (below corpus) · ncolors **8** (above corpus)
- 97% Z-twist. 85% deep_sparse_mixed.
- **Fingerprint:** extremely categorical values, many zeros, identity-tier dominant (more colors, less grammatical). The warehouse tracks *many distinct things* in *small quantities* — consistent with a storage facility logging diverse goods rather than bulk amounts.

### Ica (n=17) — the flat tally
- cat% 34 (below corpus) · max 300 · gram% 54 (below corpus) · maxlvl **1.0** (no hierarchy)
- 100% Z-twist. 71% large_value_flat.
- **Fingerprint:** flat (no subsidiaries at all), quantitative, few colors. Bulk tallies without hierarchical structure — the simplest document form in the corpus.

### Nazca (n=7) — the twist anomaly ⚠️ low-n
- cat% 78 (above corpus) · max **21** (tiny) · **45% S-twist** (unique)
- **Fingerprint:** small values, S-twist enriched. The twist anomaly is the most distinctive site-specific signal in the dataset — but n=7 demands caution. If confirmed with more data, Nazca may have a local twist convention (or a different scribal school).

## 6. Overall: Does Structure Follow Site?

**Yes, substantially.** The four major sites have clearly distinct structural fingerprints:

| | Pachacamac | Incahuasi | Ica | Nazca |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Values | Large/quantitative | Tiny/categorical | Medium/quant | Tiny/categorical |
| Colors | Grammatical-heavy | Identity-heavy | Few, flat | Standard |
| Hierarchy | Normal (L2) | Normal (L2) | **Absent (L1)** | Normal (L2) |
| Twist | Z (98%) | Z (97%) | Z (100%) | **S (45%)** |
| Cluster | Mixed | Sparse | Flat | Sparse |

**Implications for "universal grammar" vs "local practice":**

1. **The grammatical *channels* are universal.** Every site uses the seven channels (number, identity, case, evidentiality, chronology, register, composition). No site lacks a channel entirely.

2. **The channel *weightings* are local.** Pachacamac foregrounds grammatical role; Incahuasi foregrounds identity; Ica drops hierarchy entirely; Nazca shifts twist direction. These are dialect-like variations in how the shared grammar is deployed.

3. **The checklist pattern is widespread, not local.** 36 khipu across 13 sites. It's a document type (or mode) in the shared repertoire, not a Pachacamac invention.

4. **The "unknown" provenances (n=81) are the wild card.** They average close to corpus medians, suggesting they're drawn from multiple sites. Sourcing them would sharpen every fingerprint above.

**Bottom line:** The grammar is the operating system — same everywhere. The sites are running different applications on it. Context doesn't override structure; it *selects* which parts of the structure to emphasize. This is exactly what "context dictates structure" predicts: the shared grammatical competence, deployed differently by local administrative need.
