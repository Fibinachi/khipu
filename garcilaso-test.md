# Testing Garcilaso de la Vega (1609): Do Quipu Cord Colors Encode Commodities?

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Hypothesis under test:** Garcilaso de la Vega, *Comentarios Reales* (1609), claimed Inca quipu used color-coding by commodity: **yellow cords = gold, white cords = silver, red cords = soldiers/warriors.**

**Method:** Extract quipu with yellow (SY/YY/0Y), white (W), and strong red (SR/VR/RM) cords from the Open Khipu Repository (619 quipu), map their archaeological provenience, and cross-reference with known Inca resource geography.

---

## 1. Yellow = Gold: UNTESTABLE

**Data:** Only 9 quipu in the entire database contain true yellow cords (SY/YY/0Y), totaling ~33 cords. True yellow (SY, strong yellow) is vanishingly rare: 3 cords across 2 quipu.

| Quipu | Yellow shade | Cords | Provenience |
|-------|-------------|-------|-------------|
| AS069 | SY (strong yellow) | 1 | Lluta Valley, Chile |
| UR096 | SY (strong yellow) | 2 | unknown |
| AS054 | YY (pale yellow) | 6 | Hacienda Ullujalla, Ica |
| AS047/HP035 | YY (pale yellow) | 5 | unknown |
| UR283 | YY (pale yellow) | 2 | Huaca San Pedro, Armatambo (Lima) |
| UR263 | 0Y (dark orange-yellow) | 12 | Incahuasi (coastal storehouse) |
| UR266 | 0Y (dark orange-yellow) | 1 | Incahuasi |
| UR271 | 0Y (dark orange-yellow) | 2 | Incahuasi |
| AS080 | 0Y (dark orange-yellow) | 2 | (no provenience, Paris) |

**Geographic test:** Inca gold-mining regions were Carabaya (Puno highlands), the upper Marañón, and the Ecuadorian highlands. **Zero quipu in the database come from any of these regions.** This is a preservation bias — highland organics rot; coastal desert preserves — not evidence for or against the claim.

**Verdict:** The claim cannot be evaluated. The sample of yellow-cord quipu is tiny (n=9), all are from coastal or Chilean proveniences, and no quipu from gold-producing regions survive to test against. Gold is also portable — tribute gold would be recorded wherever it was received, not necessarily where it was mined. Inconclusive, not falsified.

---

## 2. White = Silver: UNTESTABLE AS STATED

**Data:** White (W) appears in 15,125 cords across the database. It is the natural color of undyed cotton — the default fiber, not a marked category. 34 quipu are >80% white.

| Provenience | Predominantly-white quipu |
|-------------|--------------------------|
| Leymebamba (highland) | 3 |
| Pachacamac (coast) | 5 |
| Ica / Ica-Pisco (coast) | 4 |
| Other / unknown | 22 |

**Problem:** Inca silver-mining regions were Porco/Potosí (Bolivia), Huancavelica (central highlands), and Hualgayoc/Cajamarca (north highlands). **Zero quipu in the database come from any of these regions.** Same preservation bias as gold.

**Deeper problem:** White is not a *choice* in the way yellow or red is. Undyed cotton is white. A quipucamayoc using white cords is using the default material. For "white = silver" to be meaningful, we would need to show that silver-recording quipu are *disproportionately* white compared to a baseline — but the baseline is already overwhelmingly white. The signal cannot be separated from the medium.

**Verdict:** Untestable. White is the default fiber color, and no quipu survive from silver-mining regions. The claim is structurally unfalsifiable with current data.

---

## 3. Red = Soldiers/Warriors: PARTIAL SUPPORT, REFINED

**Data:** 42 quipu contain strong red cords (SR/VR/RM). Unlike yellow, this is a usable sample.

**Geographic test:** Using frontier location as a proxy for military presence:

| Zone | Quipu with strong red | Total quipu | Rate |
|------|----------------------|-------------|------|
| Frontier (Lluta Valley, Arica, Leymebamba/Chachapoyas, Mollepampa) | 8 | 32 | **25.0%** |
| Pachacamac (major religious/admin center) | 16 | 90 | **17.8%** |
| Other (non-frontier coastal) | 9 | 274 | **3.3%** |

Frontier vs non-frontier enrichment: **OR = 4.62, p = 0.0039** (Fisher's exact). Statistically significant.

**The frontier sites:**
- **Lluta Valley / Arica** (far south, modern Chile): the southern frontier toward Collasuyo. Inca military presence securing the southern road.
- **Leymebamba** (Chachapoyas, northern highlands): conquered late by the Inca, a persistent frontier zone. 5 of the 8 frontier-red quipu are from here.
- **Mollepampa** (Chile): far southern imperial presence.

**Caveats:**

1. **Leymebamba confound.** Leymebamba contributes 5 of 8 frontier-red quipu, but it is *both* highland *and* frontier. The red enrichment could reflect highland ecology (as in our earlier color-zone analysis) rather than military presence. The two variables cannot be separated with n=1 highland site.

2. **Pachacamac problem.** Pachacamac was the empire's most important coastal oracle shrine — a religious center, not a military garrison. Yet 17.8% of Pachacamac quipu contain strong red, far above the 3.3% background. If red = soldiers specifically, this is unexpected.

3. **"Soldiers" is hard to test archaeologically.** Frontier location is a proxy, not a direct measure. We cannot confirm that red-cord quipu at Lluta Valley actually recorded troop counts versus tribute, labor levies, or other frontier administration.

**Refined hypothesis:** The data are more consistent with **red = state/imperial presence** than with red = soldiers specifically. Frontier garrisons *and* major administrative-religious centers (Pachacamac) both show elevated red. Red may mark the state's extractive apparatus — military, administrative, and tributary — rather than soldiers alone. This is a weaker claim than Garcilaso's but better fits the geographic distribution.

---

## Summary Verdict

| Garcilaso claim | Verdict |
|----------------|---------|
| Yellow = gold | **Untestable.** n=9 quipu, none from gold regions; no gold-region quipu survive at all. |
| White = silver | **Untestable as stated.** White is the default undyed fiber; signal inseparable from medium. No silver-region quipu survive. |
| Red = soldiers | **Partial support, refined.** Red is significantly enriched at frontier zones (p=0.004), but also elevated at Pachacamac (non-military). Better interpretation: red marks state/imperial presence broadly, not soldiers specifically. |

**The 400-year-old hypothesis survives contact with data in its weakest form and fails in its strongest form.** Garcilaso was right that colors encode *something* systematic — our earlier ecological analysis confirms this independently. But his specific commodity mappings (gold, silver) cannot be tested with surviving provenience data, and his military mapping (red = soldiers) needs broadening to state presence generally.

**What would resolve this:**
1. Quipu from highland mining regions (unlikely — preservation bias is structural).
2. The Inkawasi quipu-to-commodity mapping (which cords were found with beans vs chili vs peanuts) — direct commodity association from controlled excavation.
3. Colonial-period quipu with Spanish annotations identifying commodities — the Santa Valley tribute census approach extended to color.

---

## Limitations (honest)

- **Preservation bias is the binding constraint.** The quipu record is overwhelmingly coastal (Peruvian desert) plus one highland site (Leymebamba). The entire highland mining zone — where gold and silver were actually extracted — has produced essentially zero surviving quipu. This is not fixable with existing data.
- **Colonial mining ≠ Inca mining.** Spanish colonial mining (especially Potosí after 1545) operated at different locations and scales than Inca mining. Using colonial mining geography as a proxy for Inca-period resource zones introduces anachronism.
- **Garcilaso's reliability.** Garcilaso was writing 70+ years after the conquest, from Spain, based on childhood memories and secondhand accounts. He is a valuable but imperfect witness. His color claims may reflect a simplified or garbled version of a more complex system.
- **Color perception.** The Ascher color codes (SY, YY, 0Y, etc.) are modern standardized assessments of 500-year-old dyed fibers. Fading, soil staining, and conservation treatments may have shifted original hues. What we code as "yellow" may not match what the Inca saw.
- **n=9 for yellow.** The yellow sample is too small for any statistical claim. This section is descriptive, not inferential.
