# The Census Form: Structural Typology and Corpus-Wide Search

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Reframe (Charles):** Not decipherment (matching names to Spanish documents).
Document typology: identify the *census form type* structurally, then find
all census-type quipu across the empire.

## 1. The structural signature of a census quipu

Derived from the six Santa quipu (1000270–1000275), the only quipu with
a published census match (Medrano & Urton 2018, 1670 revisita of
San Pedro de Corongo):

| Feature | Santa value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 6-block color pattern | 67–100% of pendants (mean 86%) | 6 cords = 1 person |
| Structural levels | Exactly 2 (pendants + subsidiaries) | Person + detail entries |
| Per-cord values | Max 1–10 knots | Tax amounts, not bulk totals |
| Subsidiary sums | 1–19% match parent | Detail entries, NOT subtotals |
| Recto/verso | Mixed across set (some all-R, some all-V, some mixed) | Moiety marking (hanan/hurin) |
| E-E-E markers | Zero on all six | No entry labels |
| Form type | All Form E (hierarchical ledger) | — |
| Knotless headers | Present (opening blank cords) | Title/format rows |

**Proposed census signature:** ≥50% of pendants in same-color 6-blocks
+ max ~10 knots per cord + subsidiaries that do NOT sum to parents
+ 2 structural levels.

## 2. Are all Form E quipu censuses? No — census is a sub-type

110 Form E quipu in corpus. Only **10** meet the census signature (≥50%
6-block). The other 100 have 6-block fractions below 0.41, most below 0.16.

**What distinguishes census-Form-E from non-census-Form-E:**

| Feature | Census-like (n=10) | Other Form E (n=100) |
|---|---|---|
| 6-block fraction | 0.55–1.00 | 0.00–0.41 |
| Mean max knots/cord | 5.8 | 10.0 |
| Max knots/cord (max) | 10 | 24 |
| Subsidiary sum rate | 1–19% | (not tested corpus-wide) |
| Depth | All exactly 2 | All exactly 2 |

The 6-block fraction shows a natural break: 0.55+ (census-like) vs ≤0.41
(others). This is not a continuum — it is two populations.

## 5. Geographic distribution: coastal, not empire-wide

Census-like quipu by region:
- **Santa Valley (north-central coast):** 6
- **Ica (south coast):** 2 (Form E) + 1 (Form C)
- **Pachacamac (central coast):** 1
- **Paracas (south coast):** 1 (Form F)
- **Unknown provenience:** 1 (Form E) + 2 (Forms F, B)

**No highland census quipu identified.** All candidates are coastal.

Possible explanations:
1. **Colonial phenomenon:** The Santa six are mid-colonial. Census quipu
   may be a Spanish-administration adaptation, not an Inca original.
2. **Preservation bias:** Coastal desert preserves quipu; highland does not.
3. **Different highland format:** Highland censuses may use a different
   structural signature we have not yet identified.

No "census belt" — candidates are scattered along the entire coast
from Ancash to Ica/Paracas. The form was available empire-wide (at least
on the coast); usage was local.

## 6. What we CAN do without Spanish documents

- **Person-count estimates:** (pendants in 6-blocks) / 6 = minimum persons
  recorded. Santa: ~133 (matches published 132 tributaries).
- **Moiety splits:** R/V ratios give hanan/hurin proportions per quipu.
- **Tax distributions:** per-cord knot counts give the tax amount
  distribution across the tributary population.
- **Cross-quipu comparison:** do different census quipu show different
  tax rates, different moiety ratios, different population sizes?
- **Form census:** how many census-type documents survive per region?

## 7. What we CANNOT do without Spanish documents

- **Verify names:** color-to-name mapping requires the matching revisita.
- **Confirm "census" vs. other person-lists:** a 6-block person-list
  could be a tribute roll, labor roster (mita), military levy, or
  parish register — structurally similar, functionally different.
- **Date the quipu:** no age data on non-Santa candidates. They could
  be Inca, early colonial, or mid-colonial.
- **Confirm the 6-block = 1 person rule** outside Santa.

## 8. Known revisitas that could match

method: find a published or archival tributary list from the same
region and period, then test cord counts against name counts.

## 9. The census as a bureaucratic form

**Proposed definition:** The Inca/colonial census quipu is a
two-level hierarchical ledger (Form E sub-type) in which:

1. Each tributary is recorded as a **6-cord group** (color = identity bucket)
2. **Cord attachment side** (recto/verso) marks moiety affiliation
3. **Subsidiary cords** carry detail entries (tax components, not subtotals)
4. **Per-cord values are small** (single-digit to low double-digit knots)
5. **No E-E-E markers** (no entry labels needed — the person IS the entry)

This is a **person-register**, not a commodity ledger. The 6-block is
the diagnostic feature — it is the census form's equivalent of a
ruled row in a modern spreadsheet, marking "one row = one person."

**Corpus impact:** ~10 of 619 quipu (1.6%) are census-type. If this
ratio held empire-wide, censuses were a small but standardized part
of the bureaucratic output — consistent with periodic (not continuous)
enumeration.

## Limitations

- 6-block threshold (≥0.50) is empirically derived from the Santa gap,
  not theoretically grounded. A larger confirmed-census sample could
  shift it.
- NUM_TURNS / knot ordinals empty for most quipu — value comparisons
  use knot counts, not decoded decimals.
- No age data on non-Santa candidates — colonial vs. Inca undetermined.
- "Census" label is provisional — could be tribute roll, mita roster,
  or other person-list without documentary confirmation.
- Cross-form candidates (C, F, B) may represent different uses of
  6-grouping, not censuses.

## 10. ADDENDUM: Composite documents — census sections within larger quipu

**Date:** 2026-10-10 (addendum)
**Reframe (Charles):** Quipu can be composite documents. A single quipu
might contain a census section + a commodity section. Look for section
boundaries WITHIN quipu, not just whole-quipu classification.

### Section boundary signals (corpus-wide, quipu with L1≥30, n=328)

| Signal | Quipu count | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Strong-red singleton divider | 20 | General section break |
| Recto/verso flip (R→V or V→R) | 30 | General section break |
| Both | 2 | — |

Section boundaries are systematic. Red singletons and R/V flips both
mark "new section" — but neither is census-specific.

### Embedded census sections found

**A. 6-block runs within non-census quipu** (≥3 consecutive 6-blocks,
overall quipu 6-block <50%):

| Quipu | Site | Section | Position | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000029 | (none), Buffalo museum | 5 blocks / 30 cords | cords 89–118 of 201 | mid-document |
| 1000292 | Nazca, Goteborg | 3 blocks / 18 cords | cords 273–290 of 332 | near end |
| 1000590 | unknown, Leipzig | 3 blocks / 18 cords | cords 26–43 of 62 | mid-document |
| 1000345 | (none), Lima | 2× 3 blocks / 18 cords each | cords 12–29, 51–68 of 87 | two sections |

1000345 is the strongest composite case: TWO separate census-like
sections in one quipu, at different positions with different palettes.

**B. R/V-bounded census sections:**

| Quipu | Site | Flip at | Left section | Right section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000274 | Santa | cord 51/108 | 82% 6-block, 7 colors | 53% 6-block, 12 colors |
| 1000293 | Paracas | cord 134/145 | 63% 6-block, 134 cords | 0% 6-block, 11 cords |

### Critical finding: the Santa "Rosetta" quipu is composite

1000274 — one of the six Medrano & Urton census quipu — has an internal
R/V flip at cord 51 dividing a strongly census-like section (82%
6-blocked, 7 colors) from a weaker section (53% 6-blocked, 12 colors,
fewer subsidiaries).

This means:
1. Even the published "census" quipu are not pure census documents.
2. Medrano & Urton's person-count (132 tributaries across 6 quipu) may
   aggregate across sections with different formatting.
3. The 6-cords-per-person rule may apply to sections, not whole quipu.

### R/V flips are general, not census-specific

Leymebamba quipu (5 examples) have sharp R/V flips with 0% 6-block on
both sides. The flip marks "new section" in the scribal repertoire —
census sections are one USE of the flip, not its definition.

### Red dividers are general, not census-specific

20 quipu have strong-red singleton dividers. Red-bounded sections do
NOT differ systematically in census-likeness. Red = "section break,"
not "census boundary." (Consistent with red-tag-test.md: red singletons
are dividers/formatting markers.)

### Revised census count

| Category | Count |
|---|---|
| Whole-quipu census-like (Form E) | 10 |
| Cross-form census-like | 4 |
| Embedded census sections (new quipu) | 3 (1000029, 1000292, 1000590) |

| **Total quipu with census content** | **17** |

### The composite model

A quipu is not necessarily one document. It is a **cord-bound file**
that can contain multiple sections, each potentially a different form
type:

- Section boundaries marked by: red singletons, R/V flips, color shifts
- A single quipu may contain: census section + commodity section +
  summary section
- The "form type" classification applies to SECTIONS, not just whole quipu
- Future work: section-level classification of all 328 large quipu

### Limitations

- 6-block run detection requires CORD_ORDINAL ordering; some quipu may
  have disordered cord sequences.
- Section boundary detection used simple thresholds (R/V flip at >70%
  dominance shift); subtler boundaries may exist.
- Red divider analysis found no census-specific pattern, but sample
  (20 quipu) is small.
- Embedded sections with <3 consecutive 6-blocks (<18 cords) not
  detected — small census fragments may be missed.
