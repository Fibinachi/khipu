# Commodity Identity: If Color Is WHERE, What Is WHAT?

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Question (Charles):** We've established cord color family = ecological zone of the good's origin. Can we apply this corpus-wide, and if color gives us WHERE, what gives us WHAT — the commodity identity?

## 2. Headline Finding: No Non-Color Feature Distinguishes the Three Commodities

Every measured structural feature shows **more variation within peanuts than between commodities**:

- **Cord count:** peanuts span 169–381 (a 2.3× range); chili spans 88–176; beans 92. The chili and peanut ranges overlap.
- **Subsidiary ratio:** peanuts span 0.14–1.35; the full commodity range is 0.05–1.88. No separation.
- **Depth:** all commodities reach depth 3–4. No separation.
- **Knot profile (S/(S+L)):** peanuts span 0.30–0.93; commodities overlap at 0.79–0.93.
- **Knots per cord:** peanuts span 0.8–8.2; full range 0.8–8.2. No separation.
- **Attachment direction:** mostly V across all commodities; the chili pairs are R/V-opposed (per the paper), but so is one peanut khipu (1000610).
- **Section structure:** 1–4 sections in all commodities; same-form and mixed-form combos throughout.

**Conclusion: commodity identity is not encoded in any structural feature measurable in the OKR for these ten khipu.** This is consistent with Urton & Chu's own statement about the bean khipu: *"It is presumed that this khipu was used in the accounting of black beans, although there is nothing obvious about its appearance that may indicate this."*

## 3. The 1000614 Anomaly

1000614 (peanuts) is a structural outlier among its own commodity group:

- S/(S+L) = 0.30 vs 0.87–0.93 for the other four peanut khipu
- E-knot ratio = 0.185 vs 0.021–0.058
- Knots per cord = 0.8 vs 4.0–8.2

It is L-knot and E-knot heavy, S-knot light — the inverse profile of every other peanut khipu. Possible explanations: (a) it records peanuts at a different administrative level (e.g., a summary vs. detail document); (b) a different recording convention or recorder; (c) the peanut association is weaker than the paper implies. **This khipu alone proves that even within-commodity structural coherence is not guaranteed.** Any commodity-identity theory must account for it or exclude it with justification.

Commodity khipu tend to be **larger, deeper, and more S-knot-heavy** than the average Inkawasi khipu — consistent with detailed inventory ledgers vs. smaller administrative notes. But ranges overlap on every feature; this distinguishes document *function* (ledger vs. note), not commodity *identity*.

## 5. Corpus-Wide Application: Structural Similarity Search

Applied the two most distinctive known profiles corpus-wide (457 khipu with ≥20 cords):

**Bean-like** (GG-heavy >15%, depth ≥3, L1 <80, subsidiary ratio >0.8): **5 khipu**
- 1000613 Incahuasi (the actual bean khipu — validates the filter)
- 1000327 Pachacamac, 1000353 (unprovenienced)
- 1000401 / 1000406 Ica–Ocucaje — a near-twin pair (consecutive IDs, subratios 5.05/4.95)

All are coastal sites holding GG-heavy small deep khipu — consistent with the color=origin model (highland-origin goods recorded at coastal imperial sites). These are candidates for "highland-origin commodity ledgers," but the specific commodity cannot be determined structurally.

**Peanut-like** (≥150 cords, depth ≥3, GG <5%, S-heavy >0.7): **19 khipu**, including 1000607 (peanut) — but also **1000598 (chili)**. The profile catches a chili khipu, proving it identifies a *document type* (large detailed ledger), not a commodity. Locations span Pachacamac, Nazca, Paracas, Arica, La Molina, Chuquitanta, Mollepampa.

**Honest assessment:** structural similarity search finds *document-type* matches, not *commodity* matches. It can say "this looks like a large commodity ledger" but not "this records peanuts."

## 6. So What Encodes WHAT?

Candidate mechanisms, ranked by evidence:

1. **Archaeological context (strongest — this is the actual answer for the 10).** The goods themselves, the basket, the room, the pile the khipu was buried in. The khipu doesn't need to say "peanuts" if it's sitting in peanuts — like a spreadsheet tab identified by its folder, not its cells.

2. **Header/title cords (unproven).** Knotless opening cords appear on several khipu (UR266: 2, UR269: 9, UR272: textbook 5-cord header). They may encode a title, but we cannot read them.

3. **Characteristic quantities / tax rates (untestable in OKR).** The paper reports repeating values — 15 and 10 for chili, 47 and 208 for peanuts — possibly commodity-specific deduction rates. But NUM_TURNS and KNOT_ORDINAL are NULL for Inkawasi in the OKR, so exact decimal values cannot be decoded from the database. This hypothesis needs the paper's own readings or re-examination of the physical khipu.

4. **Combinatorial color codes (unresolved).** No single color maps to a commodity, but specific two-color *combinations* might. The bean khipu's KB-W (21/32 L1 cords) + 0R (5) signature is unique among the ten, but with n=1 for beans this cannot be generalized.

5. **Position in the archive (untestable).** Which room, which shelf, which storage unit — the khipu's findspot within the storehouse may identify the commodity zone. The peanut khipu cluster in kallanka U.A. 4; the chili in U.A. 7; beans on the U.A. 5 floor. But this is context again, not encoding.

6. **Nothing — the keeper knows (possible).** Like the Santa census keeper who knows which color is "José," the Inkawasi keeper may simply know that *this* khipu is the peanut book. The identity lives in the keeper's head and the archive's organization, not in the knots.

**The Santa analogy, refined:** for people, color = name (the identifier slot). For goods, color family = origin (the where slot). The "name" slot for goods — the WHAT — appears to be filled by **context** (physical association, archive position, keeper knowledge) rather than by any feature of the khipu itself. The khipu records *quantities of something*; the *something* is established outside the khipu.

## 7. Limits (Read Before Citing)

- **n=10**, all from one site (Inkawasi), one period (Late Horizon), one institutional context (imperial storehouse). Generalization beyond this is speculative.
- **n=1 for beans.** Every "bean-like" claim rests on a single khipu.
- **No decimal decoding possible** from OKR for Inkawasi (NUM_TURNS/KNOT_ORDINAL NULL). The most promising WHAT-hypothesis (characteristic quantities/tax rates) cannot be tested in the database.
- **1000614 falsifies within-commodity coherence.** Any future commodity-identity claim must explain or exclude it.
- **The chili "inferred" pair (UR255/256)** is itself an inference from the paper, not a direct archaeological association. If that inference is wrong, the chili sample is n=2.
- **Similarity-search hits are document-type matches**, not commodity identifications. Do not report "19 peanut-like khipu" as "19 khipu recording peanuts."

## 8. Bottom Line for Charles

Color gives WHERE (origin zone, 86–93% accuracy). Nothing in the khipu's measurable structure gives WHAT — not form type, not subsidiary pattern, not knot profile, not cord count, not section structure, not attachment direction. For the ten ground-truth khipu, the commodity is known **only** from archaeological context.

This is not a failure of the analysis; it may be the finding. The Inca system appears to separate the two questions: the khipu encodes *how much* and *from where*; the *what* is carried by the archive — the goods, the room, the keeper. A khipu is a page; the commodity is the chapter heading written on the folder.

The actionable next step is not more structural measurement of these ten — it is **decimal decoding** (exact knot values from the physical khipu or the paper's readings) to test whether characteristic quantities or tax rates identify commodities numerically.
