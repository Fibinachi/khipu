## What the literature says these six record

### The 1670 revisita link (proposed 2011, Urton)
A 1670 Spanish colonial *revisita* (census/revisit) of **San Pedro de Corongo** lists **132 named tributaries** divided among **six *pachacas*** (lineage groups, roughly equivalent to ayllus). This closely mirrors the **133 six-cord groups** in the quipus.

**Each six-cord group = one individual tributary.**

### What each cord encodes (Medrano & Urton 2018, *Ethnohistory* 65(1):1–23)
- **Cord colors** → individual's first name (color-to-name mapping)
- **Attachment knot type** → social group / status
- **Knot values** → amount of tribute paid or owed (the summed knot values of the first cord in each six-cord group correspond to tribute amounts in the census)
- **Cord group** → name, moiety affiliation, ayllu, and tax owed/paid per individual

### Moiety encoding via attachment direction
- **2018 (Medrano & Urton):** recto vs verso pendant attachment encodes moiety affiliation
- **2024 (FitzPatrick, *Ethnohistory* 71(4):443–469):** stronger alignment — **verso = *hanan* (upper moiety), recto = *hurin* (lower moiety)**; the six pachacas follow a bipartite and tripartite hierarchical structure consistent with known Andean social models

### "Rosetta quipus"
Some researchers call these the "Rosetta quipus" — the strongest known quipu-document match, potentially key to broader decipherment.

## What this means for our work

1. **The 6-block question is answered.** Charles asked what information the 6-block conveys that is "frequently available in Santa but not elsewhere." Answer: **each six-cord group is one person.** The 6-block is a *census record format* — six cords per tributary encoding identity, social affiliation, and tax obligation. Santa needed this format because these are tribute/census documents; other sites recording commodities or storehouse inventories had no use for it.

2. **Our recto/verso finding is independently confirmed.** We found two recto-heavy, three verso-heavy, one mixed among the six. The literature says this encodes hanan/hurin moiety. Our structural observation matches their decipherment.

3. **Form E = census/tribute ledger (at least at Santa).** All six are Form E (hierarchical ledger). Combined with the literature, we can now propose: Form E documents include colonial tribute censuses with a standardized six-cord-per-person format.

4. **We can build directly on the decipherment.** The 1670 revisita names 132 individuals. If we can obtain the revisita, we can attempt to match specific cord groups to named tributaries — testing and extending Medrano & Urton's color-to-name mapping.

## Caveats
- Full text of Medrano & Urton (2018) was not accessed; details above are from secondary summaries (Wikipedia, Harvard Gazette, Atlas Obscura, Google Arts & Culture). Direct paper access would firm up the cord-to-field mapping.
- FitzPatrick (2024) refines the moiety alignment; its full argument was not reviewed.
- Our knot-value decoding for these six used simplified methods; exact decimal values would allow direct comparison to the revisita's tribute figures.
- The 1670 revisita document itself was not located in this check.
