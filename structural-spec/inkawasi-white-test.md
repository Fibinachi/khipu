# Inkawasi White-Cord Test: Does the Storehouse Count People?

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Question:** Do the Inkawasi storehouse quipu show the white-cord-small-number pattern?

## Background

Corpus-wide, white (undyed) cords with values 1–4 are the most common color-value combination. The inference: these are census entries — counting people. But that's circumstantial. This test uses Inkawasi as a control: a coastal state storehouse for bulk goods (beans, chili, peanuts), excavated in situ. A goods storehouse should not be doing census. If white 1–4 cords are rare at Inkawasi, the census interpretation gains support. If common, it's challenged.

## Method

52 Inkawasi quipu identified by provenience ("Proyecto Arqueologico Incahuasi"). Cord values decoded per Ascher rules from the OKR `knot` table (S = place value, L = turns, E = 1). Compared white-cord value distributions: Inkawasi vs. all non-Inkawasi quipu.

## Results

| | White cords (with knots) | White with value 1–4 | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inkawasi (52 quipu) | 848 | 43 | **5.1%** |
| Rest of corpus (537 quipu) | 7,852 | 2,264 | **28.8%** |

Inkawasi white cords are **5.7x less likely** to carry values 1–4 than quipu elsewhere.

What Inkawasi white cords *do* record: bulk quantities. Top values include 15,031; 13,162; 12,600; 11,950; 11,680 — five-figure commodity totals, not headcounts.

## Interpretation

The storehouse doesn't count people — it counts goods in bulk. The white-cord-small-number pattern is nearly absent exactly where census activity would be unexpected, and dominant everywhere else. This is the predicted result if white 1–4 = census.

**Caveat:** This supports but does not prove the census interpretation. White could be the default cord for any common low-count category. But the Inkawasi control behaves exactly as the census hypothesis predicts, which is more than coincidence can easily explain.

## Bottom line

A goods storehouse records goods in bulk on white cords. Everyone else uses white cords for small counts. The most parsimonious reading: white is the default cord, and its most common use empire-wide is counting people.
