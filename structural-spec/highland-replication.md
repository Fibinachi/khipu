**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Task 1:** Find a second highland quipu assemblage to replicate the cord-color signal (blue/green in highland, absent on coast).
**Task 2 (Charles):** Check whether anyone has previously published the cord-color-by-ecology observation. What's novel vs confirmatory?

## Task 1: Second Highland Assemblage

### Search method

Exhaustive provenience scan of all 548 khipus in the Harvard KDB 2012 archive (`KhipuDB_22_01_12/collca_KHIPU_MAIN.sql`), plus the 101-khipu SQLite subset and the OKR 702-khipu catalog. Searched for any provenience indicating Andean highlands (>2,000m): Cuzco, Titicaca, Huancayo/Jauja, Cajamarca, Ayacucho, Huarochirí, sierra.

### Results

| Provenience | n | Zone | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leymebamba (Chachapoyas) | 22 | Highland (~2,300m) | Our original site. Only highland assemblage. |
| "Huari" (UR039) | 1 | Highland (Ayacucho region) | **Hair khipu**, Gold Museum. See below. |
| "Cuzco" (UR138) | 1 | Highland | Museo Regional de Ica. Essentially no data (1 color record, 0 knots). |
| All others | ~524 | Coastal or unknown | Ica, Pachacamac, Nazca, Pisco, Lima, etc. |

### The Huari quipu (UR039) — closer look

- **Material:** Hair (museum description: "hair khipu, Gold Museum" — Museo Oro, Fundación Miguel Mujica Gallo). Not cotton.
- **Knots:** Zero recorded. Not a standard numerical administrative khipu, or knots were not documented.
- **Cords:** 45 distinct cords, 360 color-segment records.
- **Colors:** CB (dark grayish brown) 102, SR (strong red) 90, 0B (moderate olive brown) 80, **PB (deep blue)** 54, **VB (vivid dark greenish blue)** 34.
- **Blue presence:** 44 of 45 cords contain blue (PB or VB).

This is **consistent** with the highland-blue signal — a highland quipu using blue heavily. But it is **not a statistical replication**:

1. n=1 quipu (cannot do per-khipu presence/absence tests).
2. Different genre entirely: hair khipu, no knots, likely ritual/commemorative rather than administrative.
3. Different recording conventions may apply.

It is suggestive corroboration, not confirmation. The blue-highland association holds in a second highland object of a different type, which is interesting but cannot disambiguate "highland ecology" from "highland scribal culture" the way a second administrative assemblage could.

### The Cuzco quipu (UR138)

One color record, zero knots, no usable data. Dead end.

### Honest conclusion for Task 1

**No suitable second Inca-period highland administrative assemblage exists in any standardized, published dataset.** This is a preservation problem, not a research oversight:

- Highland organic materials (cotton, wool, hair) decay in humid mountain conditions.
- The coastal desert preserves; the sierra does not.
- Leymebamba survived because the quipu were sealed in dry funerary chullpas at Laguna de los Condores — an exceptional context.
- Salomon (2013) already flagged this: the predominance of cotton quipu "may just be an artifact of better preservation on the cotton-using desert coast."

**What fieldwork is needed:** A second highland assemblage would most likely come from (a) unpublished museum collections with highland provenience, (b) new excavation of highland chullpa/tomb contexts, or (c) the patrimonial highland corpora (Rapaz, Tupicocha, Mangas) — but those are colonial-period and different genres, so they'd test the durability of the signal across time rather than replicate the Inca-period finding directly.

## Task 2: Literature Check — Has Anyone Published This Before?

**Question:** Has anyone published the observation that cord colors pattern by ecological/geographic zone (highland = blue/green, coastal = brown/yellow, with systematic cross-absence)?

### What the literature says

**Frank Salomon (2013), "The Twisting Paths of Recall":**
- Notes the cotton/coastal vs wool/highland **material** divide, but frames it as preservation bias: cotton predominance "may just be an artifact of better preservation on the cotton-using desert coast."
- Notes "dyed colors (particularly blue) occur" — blue is acknowledged as a dye color, but no zone association is drawn.
- **Verdict:** Related observation (material divide), but not the color-zone pattern. No statistical test.

**Garcilaso de la Vega (1609), via Livermore (1966):**
- "The colors showed what subject the thread was about, such as yellow for gold, white for silver, and red for warriors."
- **Verdict:** Commodity hypothesis, not ecological zone. No geographic comparison.

**Gary Urton (on Inkawasi, via History.com):**
- "We can look at how the chili pepper khipu differs from the peanut khipu and from the corn khipu in terms of their color and other characteristics and we can build up a kind of sign vocabulary."
- **Verdict:** Pursuing color-commodity associations *within* a single coastal site. Not a cross-zone comparison.

**Medrano & Urton (2018), Santa Valley:**
- Colors encode taxpayer categories (first names: light brown = Joses, dark brown = Felipes).
- **Verdict:** Social categories, not ecological zone.

**Sabine Hyland (Collata khipus):**
- Blue cord = phonetic "ka" (ankas = blue in regional Quechua dialect).
- **Verdict:** Phonetic hypothesis for a colonial-period highland corpus. No zone comparison.

**Mangas khipus (Salomon et al.):**
- "Mangas khipus exhibit a wider range of red and purple hues" compared to Inca-period khipu cords. Mangas is a highland (Ancash) site.
- **Verdict:** The closest to a comparative color observation — a highland corpus noted for distinctive hues. But it's a colonial patrimonial corpus, described qualitatively, with no statistical test and no highland-vs-coastal framing.

### Novelty assessment

| Claim | Prior art? | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Colors encode *something* (commodity/category) | Garcilaso (1609), Urton, Medrano | Confirmatory — old idea |
| Highland vs coastal *material* divide (cotton/wool) | Salomon (2013) | Confirmatory — but framed as preservation bias |
| Blue is a dye color that occurs in khipus | Salomon (2013) | Confirmatory |
| **Cord color palettes differ statistically by ecological zone** | None found | **Appears novel** |
| **Blue systematically absent from 183 coastal quipu** | None found | **Appears novel** |
| **Negative-correlation method (systematic absence as evidence)** | None found in khipu literature | **Appears novel** |
| Highland khipu noted for distinctive hues (Mangas) | Salomon et al. (qualitative) | Partial precedent — different corpus, no statistics |

**Bottom line:** The specific finding — statistically tested, ecologically framed, bidirectional (both presence and systematic absence) color patterning by zone — does not appear in the published literature. The closest precedents are qualitative asides (Mangas hues, Salomon's preservation note). Charles's suspicion is understandable given how long people have stared at these objects, but the zone-comparison was never run.

### Caveat on the literature search

This was a web-based search, not an exhaustive review of Spanish-language Andeanist journals or dissertations. A full novelty claim should be checked against:
- Urton's *Signs of the Inka Khipu* (2003) — the book-length treatment
- Brezine's dissertation work
- *Ñawpa Pacha* and *Latin American Antiquity* back issues
- Peruvian theses (PUCP, UNMSM) that may not be indexed in English search

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Inkawasi follow-up (strongest available):** 32 quipu excavated in situ at a coastal state storehouse, found with beans, chili, peanuts. If those quipu lack blue — as the coastal pattern predicts — it's a second independent confirmation of the coastal exclusion, from a commodity-anchored context. Urton is already working this material; the color data may be publishable.

2. **Unpublished collections:** Query the Museo Nacional de Arqueología (Lima) and regional museums (Cusco, Ayacucho) for highland-provenience quipu not yet in the KDB. Even 5–10 would allow a meaningful test.

3. **Patrimonial corpora as temporal extension:** Rapaz, Tupicocha, and Mangas are highland and have published color descriptions. They can't replicate the Inca-period finding, but if the blue/green signal persists into colonial-period highland khipu, it suggests deep continuity rather than a Leymebamba quirk.

4. **Full novelty check:** Read Urton (2003) and search Spanish-language sources before claiming novelty in print.
