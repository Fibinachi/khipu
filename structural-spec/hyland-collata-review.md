# Hyland's Collata Khipu: Review as Potential Decipherment Key

**Date:** 2026-10-11
**Question:** Can Sabine Hyland's work on 18th-century Collata khipu serve as a Rosetta Stone for Inca-period khipu?

## 1. What Hyland Claims

### The 95-sign system (Hyland 2017)

In 2015, village authorities at San Juan de Collata (Huarochirí province, central Andes) invited Sabine Hyland (University of St Andrews) to examine two khipu preserved as community patrimony. The khipu date to the late 18th century, associated with indigenous revolts against Spanish authority (1750s–1783).

**Physical description:**
- 487 pendant cords across the two khipu
- 14 colors, six animal fibers (alpaca, llama, vicuña, guanaco, deer, vizcacha)
- 95 distinct combinations of color × fiber type × ply direction
- Animal (not cotton) fiber — accepts dye better, enabling the color complexity
- Three-dimensional/tactile: fiber sources often distinguishable only by touch
- Villagers call them the "language of animals"

**The decipherment claim:**
- 95 signs falls within the range of logosyllabic writing systems (cf. Japanese kanji/kana inventories)
- Each pendant cord = one phonetic syllable (possibly mixed with logograms)
- At the end of each khipu, three-cord sequences encode lineage (*ayllu*) names

**Method of decipherment:**
1. Villagers identified one khipu as created by the Alluka lineage ("Ay-ew-ka")
2. Hyland hypothesized the final three cords = the sender's signature (ay / ew / ka)
3. The "ka" cord: blue, S-ply, llama wool — matched to *ankas*, the regional dialect word for blue (rebus-like principle: color name → syllable)
4. Applied the same three-cord positions to the second khipu: first two cords matched the Alluka-derived values ("a-ka"), third was golden-brown vicuña fiber → *paru* (Quechua color term) → "Yakapar"
5. Yakapar confirmed as one of only two lineages in nearby Casta; colonial manuscripts corroborate Yakapar involvement in the revolt

**Publication:** Hyland, S.P. 2017. "Writing with twisted cords: the inscriptive capacity of Andean khipus." *Current Anthropology* 58(3):412–419. DOI: 10.1086/691682. Open-access manuscript at St Andrews research repository.

### Related decipherment claims (khipu boards)

Separately, Hyland deciphered structural elements using **khipu boards** — colonial-era hybrid texts with alphabetic writing AND cords:
- **Knot direction:** On the 19th-century Mangas khipu board (MALI, Lima), alphabetic surnames each paired with a cord. S-knots vs Z-knots correlated with the two moieties (hanan/hurin). Published via St Andrews REF2021 impact case.
- **Ply direction:** Deciphered via Max Uhle's 1895 field notes matched to surviving khipu.
- These are arguably STRONGER evidence than Collata: the boards are true parallel texts (alphabetic name ↔ cord), not inferential decipherment.

**Other work:** Hyland 2016 (*The Chankas and the Priest*, Penn State Press) — historical, not decipherment. 2024: with Víctor Margarito documented the Jucul khipu (longest known, calendrical, similar to Rapaz).

## 2. The Associated Alphabetic Material

**How tight is the bilingual association?**

| Source | Type | Tightness |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Khipu boards (Mangas etc.) | Alphabetic names written beside cords on the same board | **Direct** — true parallel text |
| Collata chest manuscripts | 100+ colonial/early republican manuscripts (earliest 1645) stored WITH the khipu; Pedro Cajayauri's signed 1757 letter | **Circumstantial** — same box, same community, same era; no manuscript directly glosses a specific cord |
| Villager testimony | Descendants identify khipu as "narrative epistles about warfare"; name the Alluka lineage as creators | **Oral tradition** — motivated the ayllu-name hypothesis but not independently verifiable |
| Uhle 1895 field notes | Interview with khipu expert, matched to surviving khipu in Lima museum | **Retrospective** — 1935 interview about older khipu; grandson re-interviewed |

**Assessment:** The khipu boards are the closest thing to a Rosetta Stone in the Andes — but they are 19th-century, and they gloss only knot direction (moiety), not a full writing system. The Collata decipherment rests on two inferred readings (Alluka, Yakapar) with no independent textual confirmation of the syllabic values.

## 3. Urton's Critique

Gary Urton (Harvard) has not published a formal rebuttal, but his objections are on record in interviews:

1. **"My feeling is that the phoneticisation, if it's there, is a reinvention of khipus"** (New Scientist, 2018). The 200+ year gap between the Inca Empire (ends 1532) and the Collata khipu (~1783) means the system could be a colonial-era innovation, possibly influenced by Spanish alphabetic literacy.

2. **"Time is a big problem... Whether or not we can take these findings and project them into the past, that remains the big question."** (National Geographic, 2017).

3. **Regional variation or one-off:** The Collata system may be local to Huarochirí, not representative of Inca imperial practice.

4. The Khipu Historiography literature (academia.edu preprint, citing Hyland 2017) notes: "it is unclear whether their logosyllabism is a colonial invention in this region, or whether pre-Columbian animal fibre khipus from the Inka or Wari eras may be fully or partially logosyllabic."

**Notably, Hyland herself concedes the gap:** "we don't understand the link between these khipus and those dating from before the Spanish arrived" (New Scientist, 2018).

**Galen Brokaw** (Montana State, uninvolved): "If that's the case, that would be groundbreaking. The challenge is to find more sources... more extensive evidence."

## 4. Database Status

**The Collata khipu are NOT in OKR/KDP.** Verified by direct query of the local OKR database (~/workspace/quipu-analysis/okr-khipu.db): zero records matching Collata/Casta provenance.

**Why:** They are patrimonial khipu held by the San Juan de Collata community, stored in a wooden chest, not in any museum collection. They have never been digitized into the Khipu Database Project schema (which requires cord-by-cord knot/color/position coding — and the Collata khipu's fiber-ply distinctions are largely tactile, resisting standard coding).

**Consequence:** None of Hyland's 95 signs can currently be tested against the 619-khipu OKR corpus. The structural data (knot types, colors, cord counts) for Collata exists only in Hyland's field notes and the *Current Anthropology* paper.

## 5. Transferable Principles

Even if the specific phonetic readings don't transfer to Inca-period khipu, Hyland's work establishes several structural principles directly relevant to our project:

### A. Independent information channels (HIGH VALUE)
Hyland demonstrated that **color, fiber type, ply direction, and knot direction are independent encodings** — not redundant decoration. This validates treating each as a separate variable in structural analysis, which our project already does (color vs knot-type vs position analyzed separately).

### B. Knot direction encodes social/binary information (CONVERGENT with our findings)
Hyland's khipu-board result (S-knot vs Z-knot = moiety membership) independently confirms that **knot direction is semantically loaded**. Our project found U-direction (reversed-twist) knots clustering on anomalous cords (1000201, 1000183). These converge: direction marks *marked* status — whether moiety, repair, or exception. **Testable prediction for our corpus:** if direction encodes binary social/functional distinctions, it should pattern with color-marking (our evidentiality variable), not randomly.

### C. Combinatorial sign inventories are possible in khipu (RELEVANT to "unusual characters")
The 95-sign system (color × fiber × ply) proves khipu *can* support large sign inventories through combinatorial channels. This supports Charles's "Z" argument: rare signs are legitimate characters, not noise. Our project's 93 flagged anomalies should be evaluated as potential rare characters, not dismissed.

### D. Fiber as information (NOT TESTABLE in OKR)
The Collata system's reliance on tactile fiber distinctions (alpaca vs llama vs deer) has no correlate in OKR, which does not systematically record fiber species per cord. This is a **data gap**, not a refutation — but it means any fiber-based encoding in Inca-period khipu is currently invisible to database analysis.

### E. The rebus principle (color name → syllable)
Hyland's *ankas* (blue) → "ka" and *paru* (golden-brown) → "par" readings use the rebus principle (cf. Maya decipherment). **If** Inca-period khipu encode phonetic information, Quechua color terms are the place to look. However: our corpus-wide test showed color semantics are document-type-dependent (white-as-triage is Inkawasi-local), which cautions against universal color-phonetic mappings.

### F. What does NOT transfer
- The specific 95 syllabic values: derived from 18th-century Huarochirí Quechua dialect color terms; no chain of transmission to Inca-period Cuzco Quechua demonstrated
- The epistolary function: our 593 are administrative (census, warehouse, tally); Collata are letters. Different document types may use different encoding layers
- The animal-fiber medium: Inca administrative khipu are predominantly cotton (per OKR fiber data); the color-fiber-ply combinatorial system may require the animal-fiber medium

## 6. What Verification/Falsification Would Take

**To verify Hyland's readings:**
1. **More Collata-type khipu:** Find additional 18th-century Huarochirí khipu with independently known ayllu associations; test whether the proposed syllabic values predict the correct names. (Hyland planned this fieldwork; status unknown post-2019 Guggenheim.)
2. **Manuscript crossover:** Find a colonial document that explicitly describes a cord-to-syllable mapping (the equivalent of Diego de Landa's "alphabet" for Maya — which was itself misleading, but productive).
3. **Internal consistency:** Publish the full 95-sign inventory with proposed values; test whether the same sign gets the same reading across both khipu and across positions (not just the final three cords).

**To falsify:**
1. Show the Alluka/Yakapar readings are coincidental: demonstrate that alternative three-syllable lineage names fit the same cords equally well (the "wiggle room" Hyland acknowledged for Yakapar is the vulnerability).
2. Show the 95 "signs" are actually fewer functional signs with free variation (e.g., fiber differences are material availability, not encoding).
3. Radiocarbon or archival evidence that the khipu postdate the claimed 1783 context significantly.

**The honest bottom line for our project:**
- Collata is not a Rosetta Stone for Inca-period administrative khipu. Wrong century, wrong document type, wrong medium, n=2, no independent verification of the readings.
- The khipu BOARDS are closer to a Rosetta Stone (true parallel text), but they decode only knot direction → moiety, a single binary feature.
- What transfers is METHODOLOGICAL, not lexical: independent channels, direction-as-meaning, combinatorial sign capacity, and the rebus principle as a hypothesis-generator.
- The strongest bridge between Hyland and our project is knot direction: her moiety finding and our U-direction anomaly finding point to the same conclusion — direction is semantic. This deserves a dedicated test on the 593.
