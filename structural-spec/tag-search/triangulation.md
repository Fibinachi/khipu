# Triangulation: What Shared Tags Reveal About Meanings

**Question:** For signatures shared between 1000331 and the 4 other all-three-marker
khipu, do the contexts suggest meanings? Can we triangulate what a tag *means* from
where it appears across documents?

## Method

For each shared signature, compared its structural role across the 5 khipu:
carrier frequency, proximity to section markers, positional distribution.

## Result 1: Tag roles do NOT transfer (negative triangulation)

| Signature | 1000331 role | 1000396 | 1000507 | 1000321 | 1000659 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AB//L/S | 45% carrier | 16% | 11% | 0.5% | 0% |
| W//L/S | 19% carrier | 6% | 3% | 5% | 2% |
| MB//L/S | 7%, boundary label (68% near markers) | 14.5%, NOT near markers (15%) | 4.5%, not near (0%) | 1.5%, not near (0%) | 1.8%, not near (0%) |

The single most diagnostic finding: **MB as section-boundary label is unique to 1000331.**
In no other candidate does MB cluster near AB+BG markers. The "label" function does
not transfer. Either 1000331 uses MB differently, or the other documents are different
types where MB means something else.

## Result 2: The punctuation transfers, the vocabulary doesn't

What IS shared across all 5:
- AB+BG as compound section terminator (7% of corpus — a real convention)
- BG two-type split: E/Z-knotted BG vs L/S-knotted BG (0.8% of corpus — very rare)
- WWW triplets (common, 47.5% — weak signal alone)

What is NOT shared:
- Which tag is the carrier (AB in 1000331, split AB/MB in 1000396, none dominant in others)
- Which tags sit at boundaries (MB in 1000331 only)
- Sectional structure (13 sections in 1000331, 4 in 1000396, unclear in others)

**Interpretation:** These khipu share a *formatting convention* (how to punctuate sections,
two modes of section marking) but not a *tag lexicon* (what the colors mean). This is
like five offices that all use the same memo template but write about different things
in different shorthand.

## Result 3: The BG mode split is the most promising lead

In 1000331, BG/E/Z marks sections 1–4 and BG/L/S marks sections 5–13 (with one mixed
at the end). The knot type + twist of the terminator encodes *which mode* the section
belongs to. All 4 other khipu have both BG types present.

**Hypothesis:** The BG knot-type split marks two document halves, two scribes, two
recording sessions, or two content categories — a binary flag on sections. This is
testable: in 1000396, do E/Z-terminated vs L/S-terminated sections differ in content
(color profile, value range)?

## Result 4: 1000396 (Huaura Valley) is the triangulation anchor

- Closest geographic match to Lima-area 1000331 (Huaura Valley is north of Lima).
- 63% shared vocabulary, same punctuation, 221 cords (comparable scale).
- If any khipu can triangulate 1000331's tags, it's this one.
- **Needed:** section-by-section comparison of 1000396's 4 AB+BG sections against
  1000331's 13 — do the same tags appear in the same *section types*?

## What triangulation cannot do

We cannot assign meanings to tags from this data. Shared punctuation without shared
tag roles means the abbreviations are document-specific or office-specific, and we
don't have two documents from the *same* office with *different* content (which is
what triangulation requires). 1000396 is the same *tradition* but apparently not
the same *office*.

## Recommendation

The AB+BG + BG-split punctuation system (43 khipu with AB+BG, 5 with the full split)
deserves study as a **scribal convention** — a formatting standard, not a code.
The next step is not more tag-matching but finding two khipu from the same
provenance (ideally same site) that share both punctuation AND tag roles. Huaura
Valley and the Lima area are the places to look.
