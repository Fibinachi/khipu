## 3. Key Analytical Findings

### 3a. No clean color-commodity mapping
There is no single color that means "peanuts" or "chili" or "beans" across these khipus:
- The two chili khipus (UR267A/B) are W+AB
- The inferred-chili corridor pair (UR255/256) is AB-dominated
- The five peanut khipus use diverse palettes (YB-heavy, W-heavy, NB-heavy)
- The bean khipu (UR271) is uniquely GG-heavy

**If colors encode commodities, the encoding is not one-color-per-commodity.** It may be combinatorial (color combinations), positional, or contextual.

### 3b. The GG anomaly on UR271
UR271 (black beans, coastal) has 33 GG-YB cords. Our corpus-wide analysis found GG strongly highland-associated. Three possibilities:
1. The khipu was made in a higher zone and traveled with the beans
2. GG encodes something other than ecology (a tax category? a time period?)
3. The highland-GG association is weaker than the statistics suggest

### 3c. Recto/verso opposition (from the paper, pp. 522-523)
The paper explicitly discusses attachment direction:
> "the attachment directions — designated in Urton's khipu notational system as v ('verso') and r ('recto') — are opposed, or opposite, between khipus from each of the two linked sets"

> "What may also be important about this opposition in attachment directionalities is that it provides another mechanism for checking arithmetical operations within each three-cord set of values — that is, checking a value added in one direction by subtracting that same value going in the opposite direction."

**This is directly relevant to Charles's debit/credit hypothesis.** The paper itself proposes that recto/verso opposition encodes addition vs. subtraction — functionally equivalent to debit/credit. However, our earlier test found that Inkawasi recto/verso sums do NOT balance (ratios 0.01 to 2876), which argues against a strict double-entry interpretation at this site.

The paper's Table 1 shows UR267A as v-attached and UR255 as r-attached; Table 2 shows UR267B as r-attached and UR256 as v-attached. The matched pairs have OPPOSITE attachment directions.

### 3d. Repeating values (tax/deduction hypothesis)
- UR267A/UR255: repeating value **15** (pattern: 127 / 15 / 112)
- UR267B/UR256: repeating value **10** (pattern: 329 / 10 / 319)
- UR266 (peanuts): repeating value **47**
- UR268 (peanuts): repeating value **208**

The paper suggests these may be "amounts of goods — something like a tax — that were removed from the items being stored" (e.g., "of the 127 measures of aji brought for deposit, 15 are removed for the administration of the facility, leaving 112").

## 4. What the Paper Does NOT Contain

- **No cord-by-cord color data.** Colors are mentioned only in passing ("the colors or color patterns of the matching samples coincide as well; nonetheless... we give priority to the coincidence of sequences of numbers, regardless of whether their color patterning matches"). All color data comes from the OKR.
- **No complete sample list.** The paper discusses ~10 khipus in detail but does not tabulate all 34.
- **No quipu-to-commodity table.** The associations must be extracted from the narrative text (done above).
- **No discussion of the JC-series khipus** (OKR IDs 1000625-1000647), which may be from later excavation seasons.

## 6. Implications for the Color-Commodity Hypothesis

**The strong version is dead.** There is no single color that maps to a single commodity across these ten khipus. The two chili khipus don't share a distinctive color; the five peanut khipus use different palettes from each other.

**But the weak version survives.** UR271 (beans) is uniquely GG-heavy among all Inkawasi khipus. The peanut khipus collectively favor NB/YB/W browns. The chili khipus favor plain W/AB. There may be a probabilistic or combinatorial encoding that requires more data to resolve.

**The most important finding for Charles's framework:** The paper itself treats these as accounting documents with checks-and-balances procedures, standardized units, and arithmetic verification — not as texts to be deciphered. The authors' framing aligns with the ledger-not-language approach.
