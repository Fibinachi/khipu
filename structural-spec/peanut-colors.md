## Findings

### 1. Zero blue in all five peanut quipu
No blue-family cord appears in any of the five. This matches the corpus-wide coastal pattern (0% blue in 183 coastal quipu) and supports the ecological prediction: peanuts are a warm-climate lowland crop, recorded in coastal colors.

### 2. Coastal palette throughout
Every peanut quipu is dominated by white + the brown/yellow-brown range (AB, MB, KB, NB, YB, HB). This is the same palette as the broader coastal corpus — wide brown/yellow range, no blue.

### 3. The five are NOT uniform — two subgroups visible

- **1000607 (UR266)** stands out: YB-dominant (53 cords), HB present (23) — the only peanut quipu with HB. Fewer white cords (25). Its palette is the most "yellow-brown" of the five.
- **1000612 (UR270)** is white-dominant (173 white, 45% of cords) with NB (113) second. Minimal color diversity.
- **1000614 (UR272)** is also white-dominant (196, 54%) but carries the most green of the five (22 GG-combo cords, 6.1%) — including GG:YB, GG:W, GG-NB combinations.
- **1000610 (UR268)** and **1000611 (UR269)** are mixed: NB-led (34/95), substantial white (33/80), moderate AB and MB.

The variation suggests either different recorders, different peanut varieties/processing stages, or different accounting purposes (e.g., intake vs. stored inventory vs. disbursement).

### 4. Green is trace, not structural
Three of five have small GG presence (0–6.1%). 1000614's 22 GG cords are the most, but these are all *combined* codes (GG:W, GG:YB, GG:NB) — never pure GG. This contrasts sharply with the bean quipu.

## Interpretation

1. **The bean quipu is the outlier** — 35.5% green (mostly GG:YB) against a background where no other commodity quipu exceeds 6.1%. Black beans are a highland-associated Andean crop; GG was the highland-associated color corpus-wide. This is the single strongest color-commodity link in the dataset.

2. **Peanut quipu are ecologically consistent but not commodity-specific.** They use the coastal palette (no blue, brown/yellow dominance), matching peanuts' lowland ecology — but no single color or combination is unique to peanuts. The five differ substantially from each other.

3. **No simple color=commodity code.** The peanut quipu refute the strong version: five quipu recording the same commodity use five different palettes. Whatever colors encode, it is not a one-to-one commodity label — or commodity is encoded at a finer grain (variety, processing stage, source community, tax category) than "peanuts."

4. **The honest model:** colors pattern by ecological zone (highland vs coastal) and possibly by finer categories within zones. The bean quipu's GG spike is consistent with highland origin. The peanut quipu's coastal palettes are consistent with lowland origin. But "peanut = color X" does not hold.

## Limitations

- Five peanut quipu is a small sample; all from one storehouse, one excavation.
- FULL_COLOR combos (e.g., GG:W) may reflect banded/twisted cords rather than single-hue dye — the physical meaning of combined codes needs textile analysis.
- No knot-value analysis included here; the quantitative layer may carry commodity information independently of color.
