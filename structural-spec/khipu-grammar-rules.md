# Khipu Grammar Rules: Published Literature Review

**Purpose:** Ground the structural analysis project in independently established khipu encoding rules. Every rule below carries its source. Rules are graded by evidence strength. The final section records which rules this project has independently confirmed or tested.

**Compiled:** 2026-10-11
**Scope:** Published academic literature on how khipu encode information. Chronicler accounts included only where they state structural rules.

**Evidence grades:** `ESTABLISHED` (multiple independent confirmations, uncontroversial) · `SUPPORTED` (peer-reviewed with empirical case, not yet replicated) · `DEBATED` (active scholarly disagreement) · `SINGLE-SOURCE` (one publication or informant) · `THEORETICAL` (framework proposed, no successful decipherment from it)

---

## 1. Numerical Encoding

### 1.1 Decimal positional system
**Rule:** Khipu record numbers in base 10, with powers of ten shown by vertical position along the cord; position is aligned across successive strands.
**Source:** Locke 1923 (*The Ancient Quipu or Peruvian Knot Record*, American Museum of Natural History); systematized by Ascher & Ascher 1978 (*Mathematics of the Incas: Code of the Quipu*), 1981.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED. Confirmed by internal sum relationships (cords containing sums of following cords, repeated throughout specimens — "very improbable if the knots were incorrectly read").
**Limits:** Applies to numerical khipu; narrative/anomalous specimens diverge (see §4).

### 1.2 Three knot types with fixed digit roles
**Rule:** Each cluster of knots is one digit. Three knot types: (a) **figure-eight (E)** = 1 in the units position; (b) **long knot (L)** with 2–9 turns = units digits 2–9; (c) **simple overhand knots (S)** in clusters = tens, hundreds, thousands, etc. by position.
**Source:** Ascher & Ascher 1978, 1981; diagrammed in Ascher & Ascher 1997:29, fig. 2.11.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED for Inca-period administrative khipu.
**Limits / exceptions:** A fourth type ("EE", figure-eight with extra twist) is recognized in the Aschers' system but rare. The digit 1 cannot be tied as a long knot (mechanical constraint of the knot), hence the figure-eight. Wari-period khipu have their own knot system "which scholars do not fully understand" — this rule is Inca-specific.

### 1.3 Zero as absence
**Rule:** Zero is represented by the absence of knots in the appropriate positional slot (e.g., 804 = 8s, gap, 4L).
**Source:** Ascher & Ascher 1978, 1981.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED.
**Limits:** Absence-of-evidence problem: a missing knot cluster can mean zero *or* lost/damaged knots. Disambiguation relies on positional alignment across strands.

### 1.4 Distinctive units marker enables multiple numbers per cord
**Rule:** Because the units digit is tied distinctively (long knot or figure-eight), the end of each number is unambiguous; a single cord can therefore carry several sequential numbers.
**Source:** Ascher & Ascher 1978, 1981.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED (structural consequence of 1.2).
**Limits:** Multi-number cords are the exception; most administrative cords carry one number.

### 1.5 "Number labels": digits as codes, not quantities
**Rule:** Some digit strings are not quantities but identifiers — codes for individuals, places, or things, "much as we use numbers to identify individuals, places, or things."
**Source:** Ascher & Ascher (concept of "number labels").
**Strength:** SUPPORTED as a concept; no specific code has been decoded. Context-dependent by definition.

### 1.6 Summation cords
**Rule:** Top cords (attached above the main cord) and end-of-group cords frequently contain the arithmetic sum of the pendant cords below/beside them; "sums of sums" also occur.
**Source:** Ascher & Ascher 1978, 1981; Smarthistory ("many top cords summarize the information in the pendants, as their knots often represent the arithmetical sum").
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED for the specimens where tested.
**Limits:** Not universal — many khipu lack summation cords. Our project's Inkawasi test found parent values essentially never equal subsidiary sums (see §5), so summation is a *top-cord* phenomenon, not a general parent–subsidiary rule.

## 2. Color System

### 2.1 Color banding = individual data; seriation = aggregated data
**Rule:** Along the primary cord, a series of same-color pendants followed by another color series ("color banding") encodes individual-level data (e.g., one person's goods/services); alternating single pendants of different colors ("seriation") encodes aggregated data (e.g., village summaries).
**Source:** Hyland 2016, "How khipus indicated labour contributions in an Andean village," *Journal of Material Culture* 21(4):490–509. Based on archival testimony of the last khipu specialist of Santiago de Anchucaya (khipu in use there until the 1940s) plus museum specimens.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (one informant lineage + associated specimens). Strong within its context; untested as a general rule.
**Limits:** Colonial/recent Andean practice; projection onto Inca-period khipu is inferential.

### 2.2 Cord colors encode personal names
**Rule:** In the Santa Valley (mid-colonial) khipu set, pendant cord colors correlate with individuals' first names in the matched 1670 census.
**Source:** Medrano & Urton 2018, "Toward the Decipherment of a Set of Mid-Colonial Khipus from the Santa Valley," *Ethnohistory* 65(1):1–23.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (one matched khipu–document pair). Described as the "first instance of 'reading' information from khipu attachment knots."
**Limits:** Mid-colonial, single case. Whether color–name mapping generalizes is unknown.

### 2.3 Natural vs. dyed color inventory
**Rule:** At least eight natural cotton colors were used (white, light/medium/dark brown, chocolate, reddish-orange brown, mauve); dyed colors were mainly red and blue. Camelid wool takes dye more vividly.
**Source:** Conklin 2002, "A Khipu Information String Theory" (in Quilter & Urton, eds., *Narrative Threads*), p. 64.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED (material analysis).
**Limits:** Descriptive, not semantic.

### 2.4 Camelid wool reserved for narrative khipu
**Rule:** Khipu of camelid wool were reserved for narratives, histories, and chronicles (as distinct from cotton accounting khipu), because wool takes vibrant dye.
**Source:** Spanish chronicler Martín de Murúa, via Conklin 2002:64.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (chronicler claim). Plausible given dye properties; no independent confirmation cited.

### 2.5 Twenty-four colors in the binary model
**Rule:** Urton's constructional analysis identifies ~24 distinguishable cord colors; combined with six binary variables this yields 2⁶ × 24 = 1,536 possible information units per knot position.
**Source:** Urton 2003, *Signs of the Inka Khipu* (University of Texas Press).
**Strength:** THEORETICAL. The color count is observational; the combinatorial claim is a capacity argument, not a demonstrated code. Critics note several "binary" variables apply to whole cords or whole khipu, not per-knot positions.

### 2.6 Compound color techniques
**Rule:** At least three distinct techniques produce bi-/multi-colored pendants: barber-pole spirals, mottled blending, and single cords changing color along their length.
**Source:** Salomon 2013, "The Twisting Paths of Recall" (in Piquette & Whitehouse, eds., *Writing as Material Practice*), p. 17.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED (observational).
**Limits:** Semantic difference between the techniques (if any) is unknown.

## 3. Structural / Hierarchical Rules

### 3.1 Basic architecture: main cord, pendants, subsidiaries
**Rule:** A khipu consists of a primary (main) cord to which pendant cords are fixed by half-hitches; pendants may carry subsidiary pendants, which may carry their own subsidiaries, to 10–12 levels in extreme cases. Main cord is read left-to-right from the looped end.
**Source:** Ascher & Ascher 1978, 1981 (terminology); Salomon 2013 (half-hitch attachment); Smarthistory (reading direction).
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED. Definitional for the artifact class.

### 3.2 Recto/verso attachment encodes moiety affiliation
**Rule:** The direction a pendant's attachment loop faces — recto vs. verso (obverse/reverse) — encodes the hanan (upper) vs. hurin (lower) moiety of the person recorded. FitzPatrick's refinement: verso = hanan, recto = hurin.
**Source:** Medrano & Urton 2018 (first proposal, *Ethnohistory* 65(1)); FitzPatrick 2024, "New Insights on Cord Attachment and Social Hierarchy," *Ethnohistory* 71(4):443–469 (refined alignment via combinatorics; "first-ever identification of recto and verso cord attachment knot orientations being used as a marked and unmarked sign").
**Strength:** SUPPORTED (matched census case + combinatorial refinement). The 2018 and 2024 moiety alignments differ — the alignment itself is DEBATED, though the existence of *some* binary social encoding in attachment direction is supported.
**Limits:** Single khipu set (Santa Valley). Whether recto/verso carries meaning in non-census khipu is untested.

### 3.3 Knot type at cord top correlates with social status
**Rule:** In the Santa Valley set, the knot type at the top of each pendant group correlates with the social group/status of the recorded individuals.
**Source:** Medrano & Urton 2018.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (same matched case as 2.2).

### 3.4 Construction sequence constrains information order
**Rule:** Tertiary (subsidiary) cords are attached by their looped end *after* the parent cord is knotted — knotting a parent after attaching subsidiaries would require passing the whole subsidiary mass through each knot, "a seemingly impossible task." Construction order is therefore: spin/ply → attach → knot → attach subsidiaries → knot those.
**Source:** Conklin, "A Khipu Information String Theory" (in Quilter & Urton 2010, *Narrative Threads*).
**Strength:** SUPPORTED (physical/logical constraint argument). Not a semantic rule but a procedural one with interpretive consequences: subsidiary information is necessarily *later* than parent information.
**Limits:** Inferred from construction logic, not observed manufacture.

### 3.5 Pendants grouped in sets with spatial gaps
**Rule:** Pendants are frequently grouped in sets of n cords with empty spaces between groups; groups often contain repeating color sequences. The alternative pattern — colors in bands — also occurs. Salomon suggests the two patterns reflect complementary genera such as planning vs. execution.
**Source:** Salomon 2013:17. The planning/execution reading is Salomon's hypothesis.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED (observational pattern); the functional interpretation is SINGLE-SOURCE hypothesis.

### 3.6 Subsidiaries as corrections, exceptions, subsets
**Rule:** Subsidiary cords "may be corrections, exceptions, or subsets of information" relative to their parent.
**Source:** Smarthistory (citing Dallas Museum of Art specimens, photo: Dr. Kylie Quave).
**Strength:** DEBATED. This is the additive-detail model. Our project's Inkawasi test found parent values essentially never equal subsidiary sums and values statistically independent (r=0.14) — consistent instead with subsidiaries as *separate stocks* (rider model). The correction/exception/subset reading may hold elsewhere but is not established as general.
**Limits:** See §5 (project findings).

### 3.7 Primary cord as directory/root
**Rule:** The primary cord functions as the structural root: thicker than pendants, sometimes looped for hanging/transport; pendant layout along it organizes data into sections or categories.
**Source:** General consensus; Ascher & Ascher terminology.
**Strength:** ESTABLISHED (structural fact). Semantic content of the primary cord itself (its color, knots) varies by specimen.

### 3.8 Khipu produced in duplicate; archive copy to Cuzco
**Rule:** Official khipu records were produced in duplicate, with one copy archived at the capital Cuzco.
**Source:** Spanish chroniclers, via Smarthistory.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (chronicler claim). No paired duplicates have been identified archaeologically.

## 4. Non-Numerical / Specialized Uses

### 4.1 Inkawasi warehouse equations: a = b + c with fixed tax values
**Rule:** Inkawasi (coastal Inca storage facility) khipu encode equations of the form total = fixed value + remainder, where the fixed value is commodity-specific: 10 or 15 for chili-pepper deposits, 47 or 208 for peanut deposits. Values denote standardized produce units (grid squares on warehouse floors, ~3,510 per room), not individual items. Fixed values ≈ 2% of average deposit (11% in one chili case).
**Source:** Urton & Chu 2015, "Accounting in the King's Storehouse," *Latin American Antiquity* 26(4):512–529; reported in *Science News* 2015.
**Strength:** SUPPORTED (excavated context — khipu found under crop remains; "the first time khipus have been found in the context where they were used"). The *Science* paper (Urton, Chu et al. 2005, *Science* 309:1008–1009) established the mathematical relationships.
**Limits:** Specific to Inkawasi. Our project confirmed the repeating values but found the a=b+c summation does NOT hold as a parent–subsidiary rule in OKR data (units digits unrecoverable — all Inkawasi L-knots lack NUM_TURNS; published values came from physical inspection).

### 4.2 Tomb khipu as calendrical/demographic texts
**Rule:** The largest Laguna de los Cóndores khipu (730 cords in two groups of 365) is a two-year calendar, each cord = one day; associated tomb khipu encode demographic/mortuary data.
**Source:** Urton 2001, "A Calendrical and Demographic Tomb Text from Northern Peru," *Latin American Antiquity* 12(2):127–147.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE interpretation of a striking numerical coincidence (2×365). Widely cited, not independently replicated.
**Limits:** The 365-count is suggestive but the day-to-cord mapping is inferential.

### 4.3 Collata phonetic khipus: 95-sign logosyllabic system
**Rule:** Two 18th-century khipu from San Juan de Collata encode a logosyllabic system: 95 distinct signs from combinations of 14 colors × 6 animal fibers × ply direction; final three-cord sequences spell lineage names (Alluka; Yakapar) phonetically (e.g., blue S-ply llama = "ka," cf. regional *ankas* "blue").
**Source:** Hyland 2017, *Current Anthropology*; St Andrews announcement 2017-04-20.
**Strength:** SUPPORTED within its context (community testimony + colonial manuscripts + successful name readings). Called "profoundly significant" by peers (Dransart).
**Limits:** 18th-century, post-conquest. Urton argues these are a post-collapse invention influenced by Spanish alphabetic writing and "won't help to decipher Inca twisted and knotted cords" — Inca khipu show no such cord-combination system. The Inca-period applicability is DEBATED. Only two lineage names deciphered; the bulk of both khipu unread.

### 4.4 Binary constructional code (Urton 2003)
**Rule:** Seven binary decision stages in khipu manufacture (material choice, spin/ply direction, dye, attachment recto/verso, knot direction, knot type, color class) constitute a binary information code analogous to ASCII, yielding ~1,536 addressable states per knot.
**Source:** Urton 2003, *Signs of the Inka Khipu*.
**Strength:** THEORETICAL. No successful decipherment has been produced from this framework. Critics (e.g., computer-science reviewers) note the category error: several "bits" are whole-cord or whole-khipu properties, and khipukamayuq likely read knots directly rather than via binary decomposition.
**Limits:** Hypothesis only. The project does not rely on it.

### 4.5 Tactile readability
**Rule:** Khipukamayuq could "read" khipu by touch, with eyes closed.
**Source:** Guaman Poma de Ayala (c. 1615), via Wikipedia/secondary summary.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (chronicler). Consistent with cord-feeling practice reported by Hyland (Collata villagers "insisted on handling the khipus without gloves to feel the fibre differences").
**Limits:** Anecdotal. Implies fiber/ply carried tactually accessible information.

### 4.6 Acosta's functional description (1590)
**Rule:** "Quipus are memory aids or registers made up of cords on which different knots and different colors signify different things"; they record "histories and laws and ceremonies and accounts of business" with accuracy rivaling books.
**Source:** José de Acosta 1590, *Historia natural y moral de las Indias*.
**Strength:** SINGLE-SOURCE (chronicler). The broadest contemporary functional claim; supports non-purely-numerical use without specifying mechanics.

## 5. Where Published Rules Conflict

1. **Mnemonic vs. conventional writing.** Acosta/Guaman Poma imply conventional readability; many scholars hold khipu were idiosyncratic mnemonic devices "comprehensible only to their makers." Urton 2003 argues for empire-wide conventional units; the Santa Valley match (Medrano & Urton 2018) is the strongest evidence for conventionality, but it is one case.
2. **Binary code (Urton 2003) vs. direct reading.** Urton's seven-stage binary model vs. the Ascher-style direct knot reading (plus critics' argument that khipukamayuq read knots directly, not via binary decomposition). Unresolved; no decipherment from the binary model exists.
3. **Inca-period phoneticism.** Hyland's Collata decipherment proves *a* khipu could be phonetic; Urton denies this extends to Inca-period khipu. The field is split on whether any Inca khipu is non-numerical writing.
4. **Subsidiary function.** Smarthistory/Quave ("corrections, exceptions, subsets") vs. our project's rider-model finding (independent stocks; see below). These may both be true in different document types — the conflict is only apparent if one rule is claimed as universal.

## 6. Project Confirmation Status

Rules this project has **independently confirmed** (fresh runs, 2026-10-10/11):

- **1.2 (type-aware decoding):** Validated on Santa Valley khipu — position-1 cords decode to published constants (1000270→9, 1000271→4), all high confidence. Decoder: `decoder.py`.
- **Cord ordering:** Physical order = CORD_ORDINAL, not database CORD_ID.
- **Hierarchy field:** CORD_LEVEL is the reliable hierarchy indicator. PENDANT_FROM is set on *all* cords including top-level (verified 54/54 on Santa 1000270) and is useless for hierarchy — this corrects analyses built on it.
- **Primary cord sizing:** Primary cord length correlates with pendant count at r=0.67 (n=597) — cord sized to the file's body. Pre-cut *standard blanks* hypothesis tested and rejected (no length clustering at round sizes within file types).
- **Rider model (Inkawasi):** Subsidiaries are independent stocks, not additive detail — color contrast 69.1% vs 62.7% null (p<0.001, 0/1000 reshuffles); parent–subsidiary value correlation 0.143; riders not always smaller (29% smaller / 45% equal / 26% larger, n=65). White subsidiaries median 1 vs colored 21 at Inkawasi.
- **Five document families** emerge from unsupervised clustering (n=593): blank stubs, paired color-group census format (250), large-value flat tallies (78, previously undescribed), deep hierarchical color-grouped (76), deep/sparse mixed ledgers (172). Silhouettes 0.10–0.16 (continuum with dense cores).

Rules this project has **tested and found unsupported or local-only:**

- **Compound color order = routing (project hypothesis):** FAILS at Inkawasi. Corpus-wide AB:W/W:AB asymmetry (11.3:1) collapses to 1.2:1 within the warehouse — the signal is driven entirely by non-warehouse khipu.
- **"Compounds are always subsidiaries":** FAILS as absolute (13.6% of compounds at level 1); a depth gradient holds (13.6%→34.8%).
- **Parent = sum of subsidiaries:** FAILS at Inkawasi (exact nonzero matches essentially never occur; cf. rule 1.6 — summation appears to be a top-cord phenomenon, not parent–subsidiary).
- **White = unimportant source:** HOLDS at Inkawasi, FAILS corpus-wide (white vs colored subsidiary medians 3 vs 3, n=2641/4681; no gradient outside Inkawasi). Verdict: local scribal convention, not khipu grammar. What generalizes is structural (mains > subsidiaries, median 11 vs 3, p<0.0001).
- **Red as section divider:** No support in administrative khipu (red in only 80/593; median 0% per cluster). Appears to be a funerary/Leymebamba phenomenon.

Rules **not yet tested** by this project:

- 2.1 (banding vs seriation), 2.2 (color–name mapping), 3.2 (recto/verso moiety encoding — note: OKR has attachment-direction fields worth checking), 3.3 (top-knot status correlation), 4.2 (tomb calendar), 4.3 (phonetic — not applicable to Inca-period per Urton's objection).

**Known data gaps blocking further tests:**

- Every Inkawasi long knot lacks NUM_TURNS in OKR → units digits unrecoverable; Urton & Chu's published values came from physical inspection. Any quantitative claim about Inkawasi from OKR alone is crippled.
- 41% of Inkawasi cords decode NULL under the type-aware decoder.
- 26 funerary khipu excluded from structural analysis (binary/categorical records, different document type; Urton 2001).
