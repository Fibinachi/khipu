# Marking test: do letter-cord subsidiaries mark content cords?

## Forward direction (letter as subsidiary = suffix on content parent)
Only 20 content cords in the entire 42,184-cord corpus have letter-cord
subsidiaries: 6 marked by W, 5 by A, 4 by M, 5 by K. Against 18,386 unmarked
content cords, this is negligible (0.1%). There is no marking relationship
in the suffix direction.

## Reverse direction (letter as parent = header over content subsidiaries)
- 1,372 letter cords (16%) head groups: 2,301 subsidiaries total.
- 41-50% of these subsidiaries carry nonzero values (vs 69% for other L2 cords).
- Subsidiary colors show same-family dominance (W heads W/KB/AB; A heads AB/W/MB; M heads MB).
- CONTROL: letter-headed L1 groups vs non-letter-headed L1 groups:
  - mean subsidiaries: 1.67 vs 1.82 (no difference)
  - color homogeneity: 0.307 vs 0.245 (marginal, not distinctive)
- Letter-headed groups behave like ordinary L1 parent groups. The "header"
  pattern is normal khipu hierarchy, not a distinctive grammatical function.

## Verdict
Neither direction supports grammatical marking. Letters are top-level
structural elements, consistent with the spreadsheet model (column formats /
section structure), not morpheme-like markers on content.
